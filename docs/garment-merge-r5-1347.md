# Villager garment construction — merged plan, revision 5, stage 1 (Fable 5.1)

Answers GPT-6 Astra's round-5 break check (docs/garment-check-5-1347.md, B1-B3) for stage 1 only and replaces docs/garment-merge-r4-1347.md in full; revision 5 stands alone. Stage scope is unchanged from revisions 3 and 4: gate removal, before pictures, outfit enumeration and rank table, the body pre-split, the shell builder, and the migration of trousers, breastCloth, veil, neckBeads-neck, neckBeads-chest and waistBeads. Column bones stay at the start of stage 3.

Three changes this revision makes, each the simplest sound mechanism found. The export handoff in pipeline.py forwards the shells (B1). Region membership is no longer a weight test: every body vertex carries three immutable region scalars computed before the split, height, summed arm weight and summed leg weight per side, and a shell's region is an interval in height and a bound on the arm scalar, so the trousers cover the pelvis and crotch exactly as the legacy seat tube did (B2). The split tool interpolates those scalars along the cut edge and sets the cut scalar to its level value exactly; classification reads them and never the refitted skin weights (B3).

Acceptance standard, applied throughout: visibly convincing clothing at 3 m and 7 m in the port city and the village, judged in pictures on both backends; measured numbers are detectors with a named response, not a proof of zero penetration.

## 1. Round-5 objection answers

- **B1 (export handoff drops the shells)** FIXED. pipeline.py's export branch builds `dress` as `{'meshes': {g- entries}, 'meta': garments['meta']}` and passes only that to `E.export`, so revision 4's `garments['shells']` never reached the writer. Revision 5 changes the one line: `dress` gains `'shells': garments.get('shells', {})`, and export.py's `export` reads `garments.get('shells', {})` to write `scene.extras.villager.shells` and to set `kind: 'shell'` on the six `meta` entries. The `meshes` loop is untouched, so the `g-` filter still keeps hair and eyes and every shell out of the mesh list. A gltfio selftest asserts that an export with six shells writes no mesh for them and that the extras block lists six names, six base offsets, six ranks and a face-mask array of the body's triangle count. The MK.check call after the export and the sheets step keep reading `garments` as before and are unaffected.
- **B2 (bare seat)** FIXED. Revision 4 defined the trousers' region as "girdle to hem on both legs" with the hem cut restricted to vertices of leg weight over 0.5, and the all-three-vertices rule then excluded every face with a hips-weighted corner: the pelvis, the seat and the inner thighs at the crotch, which is exactly the skin garments.py's trousers builder covers with its seat tube (`tube(B, L['girdle'], L['hip'] - 0.08 * H, ...)` over the whole trunk, before the two leg tubes from hip − 0.04 H down). Revision 5 drops the weight condition. The trousers' region is every body face whose three vertices have height in [hem, girdle] and arm scalar at most 0.2, where hem is knee − 0.5 (knee − ankle) from `landmarks` (knee is `shin.L`, ankle `foot.L`; the body is X-symmetric, so one height serves both legs) and the arm scalar is defined below. Pelvis, seat, crotch and both legs are in by height; the arms are out by the arm scalar, which is 0 on hip and thigh skin, so the hands that hang beside the thighs in the A-pose body never enter the region even where their height falls in the band. The hem is therefore an ordinary height level set cut over the whole body, not a per-leg cut, and the girdle set is the one already cut for the whole trunk. The legacy seat's hem at hip − 0.08 H is not needed: a skin-following shell has no gap between its seat and its legs. Picture acceptance keeps trousers alone, side-on and three-quarter, and adds a rear three-quarter view judged at the seat.
- **B3 (region labels from refitted weights)** FIXED. Revision 4 classified faces from "height interval and vertex set", and the vertex sets were weight thresholds on `W`, which the split's `top4` and the non-negative refit rewrite, so an inserted vertex at summed arm weight 0.2 lands on either side after the refit. Revision 5 separates classification from skinning. Before any cut, body.py computes from the full `W` returned by `build_body` three per-vertex scalars and stores them as `body['region']`: `y` (rest height, from `pos`), `arm` (sum of the six arm bone columns, as garments.py's `Body.arm`), and `leg.L`, `leg.R` (sum of thigh, shin, foot, toe per side, as `Body.leg`; kept for stage 3's leg-split skirts, unused by stage 1). These are immutable: nothing after the split writes them. The split tool, when it inserts a vertex on an edge at parameter t for the level set of scalar s at value c, interpolates every region scalar linearly at t and then overwrites s with c exactly, so the vertex is on its level set in the classification scalars by assignment, not by arithmetic. The skin weights are interpolated at the same t, then `top4`, then the refit, and may move freely in weight space; they are read only by skinning. Every region bit is set from `body['region']`, so a face is on one side of every cut by construction and the refit cannot unlabel it. The runtime reads the per-face masks from the extras and never classifies; the pipeline selftest cuts a toy triangle at arm 0.2, refits its vertex to weights with arm sum 0.31, and asserts the vertex still carries 0.2 and the two faces beside it keep their bits.

**Defects found in revision 4 by this merger.**

- **D8 Export handoff.** Revision 4's data flow said export.py "adds `extras.villager.shells`" from `garments['shells']`, but pipeline.py rebuilds `dress` from `meshes` and `meta` only, so the writer never received them (B1).
- **D9 Weight-set regions.** Revision 4's region rule used weight thresholds both as the trousers' vertex set and as the arm boundary, which excluded the pelvis (B2) and was undefined after the refit (B3). Both are replaced by the immutable region scalars.

## 2. The stage-1 plan

**Gate removal.** Unchanged from revision 3: pipeline.py drops the `raise RuntimeError('penetration: ...')` and the docstring sentence; `report` keeps returning `bad` and `unchecked`. Lands first on its own.

**Before pictures.** Unchanged: one forced outfit per acceptance row, 3 m and 7 m, both backends, village and port city; the dress sheets under verification/villager-body/ are the pipeline-side before record.

**Outfit enumeration.** Unchanged: the implementing session lists every generated (form, wear) pair and co-worn combination from `appearanceFor` and records it here; equal-rank overlaps are resolved by inserting a rank, never reordering or forbidding.

**Rank table (stage 1).** Offset is base plus rank times step, both from balance config, defaults 3 mm and 3 mm; a shell may override its base (breastCloth's named response).

| Rank | Garments |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | hairBag-crown (stage 5) |
| 1 | neckBeads-neck, breastCloth-chest, loinFlap and girdleTails bands (stage 3) |
| 2 | trousers-waist, neckBeads-chest, apron band, skirtShort, skirtKnee, wrapLong-waist (stage 3) |
| 3 | waistBeads-waist, veil-face |
| 4 | shirt, robe, both togas, wrapLong-chest (stage 4) |
| 5 | hood (stage 5) |
| 6 | capes and cloaks (stage 5) |

Rigid pieces have no rank: cap, headband, headRing, topknot, turban, limbRings, babySling. Hair cap and eyes are pipeline-only.

**Region scalars.** After `build_body` and before any cut, body.py stores `body['region'] = {'y': pos[:, 1], 'arm': W[:, arm bones].sum(1), 'leg.L': ..., 'leg.R': ...}` from the full-width `W`. These are the only inputs to region classification. They are extended by the split and never recomputed; `arm` and `leg.*` are not re-read from `W` after the split. Heights come from `landmarks(body['joints'], H)` as in garments.py, H the stature from config.

**Body pre-split.** A generic split tool in body.py cuts the decimated body along the level set of a region scalar at a value c, optionally restricted to faces inside a height interval (so a shoulder contour is cut once and a short band's rows cut only where they can exist). On every crossed edge it inserts one vertex at the crossing parameter t with position, every morph delta, every region scalar and the full weight vector interpolated at t; the cut scalar is then set to c exactly; the weights go through `top4` and then the B1 refit from revision 3 (non-negative least squares over the four bones, sum one, over the rest pose, the design pose and the plus and minus 45 and 90 degree rotations about two axes per carried bone), stored back into the full-width `W` with four nonzero entries so export.py's `top4` is the identity on them. No snap fallback: the vertex stays on its edge whatever the error. Each split triangle becomes two or three with no hanging vertex; `tri_uv` is interpolated per split corner. `body['map']` is extended by `surface_map` on the inserted positions against `basis_full`; morph deltas of inserted vertices are the edge interpolation of their endpoints' deltas, exact at every corner because the inserted point is the same edge point in every corner shape. The cuts, in order, with the whole body cut unless an interval is given:

- `y` at girdle (hip + 0.06 H); `y` at hip + 0.013 H and hip + 0.027 H (waist beads band)
- `y` at hem knee − 0.5 (knee − ankle) (trousers; one height, both legs)
- `y` at chestTop and at waist − 0.01 H (breast cloth); `y` at chestTop − 0.06 H and neck − 0.008 H (chest collar); `y` at neck + 0.002 H (neck band top); `y` at neck − 0.025 H and chin + 0.055 H (veil)
- `arm` at 0.2 (the arm boundary) restricted to faces with `y` in [hem, neck + 0.002 H], which is every stage-1 region that excludes the arms; the contour is one closed loop per shoulder plus nothing elsewhere, because arm weight is zero on trunk and leg skin away from the shoulders

Every body triangle gets a 32-bit region mask, one bit per stage-1 shell, set when all three vertices satisfy that shell's region predicate on `body['region']`: a closed height interval on `y` and, where stated, `arm` at most 0.2. Because every inserted vertex carries the level value exactly, each face lies on exactly one side of every contour and the predicate labels it consistently. The face region masks, each shell's level-set rows (as lists of body vertex indices in loop order) and its rank ship in `scene.extras.villager.shells`. The clip-frame regression over every inserted vertex and every split original triangle runs in the garments step as revision 3 describes, reported per edge against 1 mm and 3 mm. Named response at 3 mm or more: the edge is listed in the garments-step report and the implementing session widens the refit pose set for that vertex's bones; the picture decides whether the error shows.

**Shell regions (stage 1).** Predicates on `body['region']`:

| Shell | Region |
| --- | --- |
| trousers-waist | `y` in [hem, girdle], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| breastCloth-chest | `y` in [waist − 0.01 H, chestTop], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| neckBeads-chest | `y` in [chestTop − 0.06 H, neck − 0.008 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| neckBeads-neck | `y` in [neck − 0.008 H, neck + 0.002 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| veil-face | `y` in [neck − 0.025 H, chin + 0.055 H], whole head |
| waistBeads-waist | `y` in [hip + 0.013 H, hip + 0.027 H] |

**Shell builder (one recipe, two implementations).** Inputs: a body (positions, full uncut index, weights), the shell's face set, rank, base offset, and its boundary rows (its own level sets and every higher-rank shell's level set inside its region). Output: a geometry whose vertex k has a source index into the body. Positions are source plus offset along the body's area-weighted vertex normal, recomputed from the positions on the full index by `vertex_normals` in Python and the equivalent loop in TS. Each shell vertex carries its source's weights and skin indices, its source's normal as its own normal attribute, its source's `garmentPush` (the scalar from `decodeCover` with the legacy worn list, applied in the shader along `normalLocal`, so shell and source are pushed along identical skinned vectors and a rim foot never separates from pushed skin), zero relative morph targets of the shell's vertex count with the composed body's target names and `morphTargetsRelative = true` (as `gltfGarment` does; no shell reaches a hand, so zero deltas are exact), and in the pipeline additionally its source's morph deltas. A rim strip, a ring of quads from the offset row down to an un-offset copy of the same body vertices, is emitted at every boundary row that is a drawn edge: the shell's own rows always, a higher-rank shell's row only when that shell is worn and so cuts there. The rim strip is lit with the body's normal, one offset wide (3 to 12 mm), a recorded residual to look for in the pictures. The body's boundary vertices stay in the drawn faces outside the region. uv is cylindrical about the hips axis on the composed body (angle round the axis, height along it), so a fibre pattern keyed by uv keeps its scale. The shell passes through the same paint path as a legacy garment.

**Face cut, at assembly with the worn shell set and the legacy worn list.** A body triangle is cut if its region mask shares any bit with a worn shell, and independently by `maskedIndex` for the legacy mask. A shell triangle is cut if its source triangle's mask contains the bit of a worn shell of higher rank, or if all three of its source vertices are hidden by the worn legacy garments (revision 4 D7). Nothing is cut by a lower or equal rank.

**Data flow.**

- Pipeline: the garments step runs the recipe on the drawn body for the six and stores them under `garments['shells']`, outside `meshes`, so fit.py, mask.py and export.py's mesh loop never see them. pipeline.py's export branch passes `dress = {'meshes': ..., 'meta': ..., 'shells': garments.get('shells', {})}` (B1). export.py writes no mesh for a shell and adds `extras.villager.shells`: bit names, per-face region masks, ranks, boundary rows, base offsets, and `kind: 'shell'` in `garments` meta for the six. cachekeys cover the shell module and the region scalars.
- Runtime: `gltfPerson` is unchanged apart from reading the shell extras. `gltfFigureGeometry` paints the body, decodes the legacy cover once with the legacy worn list, builds each worn shell from the painted body's positions on the full index, cuts body and shell faces by the rule above, sets each shell's push from the source flags, paints the shells, merges, and copies the relative flag and target names as today. One geometry and one draw call per figure.
- Round trip: villagerRuntimePose.test.tsx exports the composed rest body (after morph bake, hang, scale and `stylisePeaks`) and every worn shell's rest positions for the test outfits; gamepath.py recomputes the shells from the exported rest body and compares within 1e-4, then applies skinning and push to both and reads the posed shell-to-body clearance as a detector: the interior of a shell is expected within one offset step of its nominal offset, rims are reported separately and not held to it, and a reading outside the band names the frame, shell and vertex. Named response: inspect that frame in the picture; a reading under half the offset on more than one frame raises that shell's base offset by one step. A second comparison against gamepath's own drawn body with a `stylisePeaks` mirror is the detector of the full chain, as in revision 3.

**Migration of the six.** Each moves as a whole mesh in one commit; reference builders as listed in revision 3 (garments.py `build_form`). After migration the six are the regions of the table above at the ranks of the rank table; the breast cloth has no fill and follows the bust contour without bridging the cleft (recorded residual; named response: raise breastCloth's base offset by one step, and if the picture still reads as painted skin the breast cloth reverts to its legacy hull-tube builder and migrates with the trunk shells in stage 4). Bands are a rank step thick and read flat, a recorded residual. `finish`, `push_out`, weight transfer, `unskin` and `surface_map` are not called for these six. GARMENTS keeps the six entries so `build_form` output order is unchanged.

**Legacy garments over and under the shells.**

- Over shells: shirt, robe, toga and wrapLong-chest over the breast cloth and collars; hood over the veil and collars; every outer piece over everything; shirt over trousers; loin flap, apron, girdle tails and skirts over the waist beads. The shell is hidden where its three sources are hidden by the legacy garment and pushed with its sources near openings. The remaining risk is a shell face at an opening standing outside the legacy cloth; the new penetration row "legacy garment against co-worn shell" measures it and the robe-over-trousers and flap-over-beads pictures judge it. Named response: raise that legacy garment's ease by one rank step until stage 3 or 4 migrates it.
- Under shells: waist beads under trousers and neck beads under the veil, by rank, with rim strips at the cut rows: the inner shell's cut edge closes to the skin with a strip from its offset to 0, the outer shell's own rim closes from its larger offset to 0 on the same body vertices, so the outer strip encloses the inner and no open edge shows.
- Body under shells: a body vertex both hidden by a legacy garment and cut by a shell's bit is absent; a body vertex pushed by a legacy garment carries its shell with it by the shared normal and push.

**Tests.**

- Pipeline selftests: the split tool on a toy triangle (interpolation, exact level value on the cut scalar, non-negative normalised four weights, region bits unchanged by a refit that moves the weights across the threshold (B3), map extension); the shell recipe on a toy mesh (offset along `vertex_normals`, rim strip at own and cut rows closing onto the un-offset row, cut rule for body and shell faces, hidden-source rule); the trousers predicate on the real body asserting that every face between hem and girdle with arm scalar 0 carries the trousers bit, pelvis and crotch included (B2); gltfio export with shells writes six extras entries and no shell mesh (B1).
- Garments step: the clip-frame regression over EXPORT_CLIPS at every CORNERS corner, per edge, 1 mm and 3 mm.
- penetration.py, no gate: shell against body posed (clearance band, rims reported apart); legacy garment against each co-worn shell. Legacy rows keep their baseline and must be unchanged within the report's noise.
- Vitest: shell identity on the composed body for every test outfit (position, normal copied from source, push copied from source, zero relative targets with the body's names, rim rows at offset zero); push composition with a legacy garment over each shell; face cut completeness including hidden-source faces; bit-order agreement; the rest-space round trip; villagerFigureBody.test.ts accepting shell descriptions; villagerGarmentMask.test.ts against the regenerated check json.

**Picture acceptance (3 m and 7 m, both backends, village and port city, forced outfits).**

- trousers alone, walk and run, side-on, front three-quarter and rear three-quarter judged at the seat (B2), adult man and child
- trousers under shirt and under robe in walk
- breast cloth in walk and dig, front-on, adult woman at the stout and slight builds, judged specifically at the cleft
- breast cloth with the chest collar, front-on, idle and walk
- veil with and without a hood, and with a cap, idle with the head turned
- chest collar and neck beads over bare skin and under a robe neckline, idle, judged at the shoulder contour for a ragged edge (B3)
- waist beads under a loin flap and under an apron in walk, and over trousers in walk

Judged for holes, cloth reading as skin, beads reading as a torus, dark marks, a rim strip lit wrong, a ragged edge at the arm contour, and a hem or neckline standing off the skin. A surviving dark mark is traced to its mesh with the debug unlit material on both backends before it is classified.

## 3. Left for later stages

- Stage 4: trunk shells plus driven hems for shirt, robe, both togas and both wraps, and the breast cloth's cleft if the stage-1 shell fails its picture; delete `tube`, `sleeve`, `armholes`.
- Stage 5: cloaks and capes with arm clearance, hood shell, head envelopes and outfit-dependent offsets, sling, limb rings, hair bag, `finish_rigid`; delete `column_sweep`, `skirt_weights`, `OUTER`, `SKIRTS`, `RIGID_HEAD`, `RIGID_CHEST` and the per-slot layer table.
