# Villager garment construction — merged plan, revision 7, stage 1 (Fable 5.1)

Answers the round-7 break check (docs/garment-check-7-1347.md, B1–B9, served on the Opus 5.5 fallback; step-4 approval still needs a GPT-6 Astra check) for stage 1 only and replaces docs/garment-merge-r6-1347.md in full; revision 7 stands alone. Stage scope: gate removal, before pictures, outfit enumeration and rank table, the body pre-split, the shell builder, and the migration of trousers, breastCloth, neckBeads-neck, neckBeads-chest and waistBeads. The veil leaves stage 1 (B2) and keeps its legacy builder until stage 5. Column bones stay at the start of stage 3.

Changes this revision makes: the veil stays legacy (B1, B2); shells carry no uv and are keyed by position like every legacy garment (B3); the pipeline builds each shell per body corner from that corner's positions and normals and ships no shell morph deltas (B4); the clearance detector measures against the shell's own source fan, and contact with another body part is its own row with its own response (B5); the six stay in `garments['meshes']` as `part: 'shell'` and every pipeline enumeration is told how to treat that part (B6); the runtime routes shell layers before `layerOf` and `gltfLayerGeometry` returns null for a shell without logging (B7); the skirts, the apron band and wrapLong-waist move above the waist beads in the rank table (B8); the runtime offset and the round trip scale by `person.frame.scale` (B9).

Acceptance standard, applied throughout: visibly convincing clothing at 3 m and 7 m in the port city and the village, judged in pictures on both backends; measured numbers are detectors with a named response, not a proof of zero penetration.

## 1. Round-7 objection answers

- **B1 (veil-face lacks the arm exclusion)** FIXED, by removal. The veil leaves stage 1 (B2 below), so stage 1 has no veil region, no veil cuts and no whole-head predicate; every stage-1 region now carries `arm` ≤ 0.2. The requirement is recorded for the veil's migration in stage 5: its region predicate carries `arm` ≤ 0.2 as the legacy builder's `B.arm < 0.2` rings do (garments.py `build_form`, `'veil'`), the 0.2 contour cut is then extended above neck + 0.002 H, and it gets the waist beads' selftest (one connected component, no face with a corner at arm ≥ 0.2). Section 3 carries the note.
- **B2 (veil as a 12 mm face-following shell)** FIXED, by keeping the legacy veil in stage 1. The objection is right on the mechanism: `push_out` (garments.py) already guards against face normals pointing into the mouth's slit, and a skin-following offset over the lips, nostrils and under-chin hollow folds where neighbouring normals converge, which is a dark mark or a see-through slit; and a face-tight shell reads as a balaclava, not a veil. No cheap bridge makes it convincing. So the veil is not a stage-1 shell: `build_form`'s `'veil'` branch, its `finish` and its cover mask stay as they are, the veil keeps its legacy rank handling (cover-mask hide and push), and it migrates in stage 5 with the hood and the head pieces, where a head envelope exists to hang a convex shell from. Stage 1 migrates five garments, not six. The veil picture row stays (veil with hood, with cap, head turned) because the neck collar and the neck beads are shells under it, and a front-on 3 m mouth and chin row is added for the hood-over-collar case.
- **B3 (shell uv breaks `mergeGeometries`)** FIXED. Verified in export.py: `add_skinned_mesh` writes POSITION, JOINTS_0, WEIGHTS_0, NORMAL (body only), `_COVER` and `_INFANT`; no TEXCOORD_0 for the body or any garment, and the garments' `g['uv']` is read only to flag the infant's head. villagerFigureBody.ts `gltfFigureGeometry` merges `parts` with `mergeGeometries(parts, false)` and throws "body and garments do not merge" on null, which three returns when the attribute sets differ. A shell with a `uv` attribute would therefore throw for every figure wearing one. Shells carry no uv. The pattern is keyed exactly as the legacy garments' paint path keys it: `gltfGarment` calls `paintSurface(g, colour, surfaceOf(l, person.p, bottom), second, fibreOf(l))` on a geometry without uv, so the shell goes through that same call on its own positions, and the fibre keeps its scale because the positions are the body's. The vitest shell-identity test asserts that each shell part has exactly the attribute names of the painted body part after `tidy` and `applyGarmentMask` (position, normal, skinIndex, skinWeight, the colour and surface attributes `paintSurface` adds, `garmentPush`), which is the merge precondition. The folding-u argument becomes moot.
- **B4 (pipeline shell differs from the runtime shell at the corners)** FIXED. Revision 6 built the pipeline shell once on the basis body and carried the source's morph deltas; the runtime offsets along the composed body's normals. At the female and child corners the normal direction differs, worst under the bust, so penetration rows and sheets measured a shell the game does not draw. Revision 7: the pipeline stores no shell positions and no shell morph deltas. `garments['meshes'][name]` for a shell holds the recipe inputs (face set, boundary rows, rank, base offset, `part: 'shell'`), and sheets.py and penetration.py call the shell recipe per body corner on that corner's drawn positions (`body['pos']` plus the corner's `morph_pos`) with `vertex_normals` recomputed there, which is the same recipe the runtime runs on the composed body. Agreement is exact at every corner the appearance table names; between corners the runtime composes bilinearly and the recipe is run on the composed shape, so the round trip (below) is the detector of the hang, `stylisePeaks` and the intermediate builds, within 1e-4.
- **B5 (clearance to another body part is not a shell-offset problem)** FIXED. The clearance detector is split into two rows. Row one, "shell against its own source": the posed distance from each shell vertex to the fan of its source vertex's incident body triangles. With identical weights the posed shell point is the posed source plus offset times the skinned normal blend, so the reading is at most the offset and drops only where the blended normals shrink it; the band is [half the offset, one offset step over the offset], rims reported apart. Named response: a reading under half the offset on more than one frame raises that shell's base offset by one step. Row two, "shell against another body part": a shell vertex whose nearest body triangle is not in its source's region face set and lies inside the body (the hung upper arm against the lateral chest, the inner thighs, a hand beside the thigh). It is reported per frame with the part's dominant bone, and its named response is never an offset change: the frame is inspected in the picture; if it reads, the fix is a contact in the clip or the stage-3/4 cut, recorded as a residual with the frame named. The legacy `armholes` cut has no shell equivalent in stage 1 because no stage-1 shell crosses the arm contour.
- **B6 (outfits, `MK.check`, the sheets and the baseline naming the six)** FIXED. Verified in pipeline.py: the export branch filters `garments['meshes']` by the `g-` prefix, then `MK.check(..., [o for _s, _a, o in S.outfits(garments)])`, and `S.sheets` and `P.report` take `garments` whole. Revision 6 moved the six out of `meshes`, so any outfit, sheet or baseline row naming them reached a missing key. Revision 7 keeps the six in `garments['meshes']` under their legacy names with `part: 'shell'`, and the treatment per reader is: outfits (`S.outfits`) enumerate names from `meshes` unchanged and keep naming the six; the export filter becomes `n.startswith('g-') and g.get('part') != 'shell'`, so no shell mesh is written; mask.py gives a shell no cover bits, leaves it out of `mask['garments']` (the runtime's `wornBits` skips a name absent from the order, which is what the shells need: a shell neither hides nor pushes the body through the cover mask) and computes the legacy garments' masks over the body exactly as before; `MK.check` skips names whose part is `shell` when it reads an outfit against the mask; sheets.py draws a shell by the recipe at the sheet's body corner, cut by region bits; penetration.py routes a `shell` part to the two shell rows and the "legacy garment against co-worn shell" row, and the baseline comparison reports the six legacy rows as `migrated` rather than `unchecked`, keyed on the part change, so they neither fail nor vanish. sheets.py, mask.py and penetration.py are not attached to this revision; the branch above is an assumption the implementing session verifies by reading each module's iteration over `meshes`, and a pipeline selftest runs `S.outfits`, `MK.check` and `P.report` on an outfit naming all five stage-1 shells and asserts no KeyError and five `migrated` rows.
- **B7 (`layerOf` drops shell layers)** FIXED. Verified in villagerFigureBody.ts: `gltfFigureGeometry` builds `drawn` from `layerOf(l)` over every layer; the default `gltfLayerGeometry` calls `gltfGarment`, which returns null when `asset.geometries[name]` is missing, logs "holds no garment" in DEV and the layer is left out. A shell layer has no mesh in the file, so under revision 6 every shell was dropped. Revision 7: `gltfFigureGeometry` partitions `layers` into shell layers (name present in `asset.shells`, read from `scene.extras.villager.shells`) and legacy layers before `layerOf` is called; `layerOf` is called for legacy layers only; shell geometries are built in `gltfFigureGeometry` from the painted body of that call and are not cached by `layerOf` (they depend on the painted body and the worn set). `gltfLayerGeometry` returns null for a shell layer before `gltfGarment` and before the DEV log, so a caller that warms its cache over all layers (skinnedFigure.tsx) neither logs nor caches a shell. villagerFigureBody.test.ts holds every generated pair to either a mesh or a shell entry.
- **B8 (skirts ranked under the waist beads)** FIXED. Revision 6's table put skirtShort, skirtKnee and wrapLong-waist at rank 2 under waistBeads-waist at rank 3 while its text and picture rows put skirts over the beads (the legacy `waistBeads` comment in garments.py builds the beads below the girdle so they tuck under what hangs from it). The table below inserts rank 4 for the apron band, skirtShort, skirtKnee and wrapLong-waist and shifts the outer ranks up. Stage 1 therefore fixes the beads' offset it will keep.
- **B9 (runtime offset must scale by `person.frame.scale`)** FIXED. Verified: `gltfFigureGeometry` computes `depth = VILLAGER_ASSET.garmentMaskPush * person.frame.scale`. Base and step are pipeline figure units at the pipeline stature; the runtime offset is (base + rank × step) × `person.frame.scale`, the round-trip export writes the scale it used, gamepath recomputes with the same factor, and the shell-identity vitest asserts the offset on a child and an adult against that product.

**Defects found in revision 6 by this merger**, all against the attached code.

- **D11** Shells with a `uv` attribute cannot merge with uv-less body and garment parts (export.py writes no TEXCOORD_0; `gltfFigureGeometry` throws on a null merge). Fixed by B3.
- **D12** Shell layers reached `layerOf` and were dropped by `gltfLayerGeometry` (B7).
- **D13** The runtime offset was unscaled while `garmentMaskPush` is scaled (B9).
- **D14** The six left `garments['meshes']` while every pipeline reader enumerates that dict (B6).

Revision 6's answers to round-6 B1 and revision 5's to round-5 B1–B3 stand and are embodied below.

## 2. The stage-1 plan

**Gate removal.** Unchanged from revision 3: pipeline.py drops the `raise RuntimeError('penetration: ...')` and the docstring sentence; `report` keeps returning `bad` and `unchecked`. Lands first on its own.

**Before pictures.** Unchanged: one forced outfit per acceptance row, 3 m and 7 m, both backends, village and port city; the dress sheets under verification/villager-body/ are the pipeline-side before record.

**Outfit enumeration.** Unchanged: the implementing session lists every generated (form, wear) pair and co-worn combination from `appearanceFor` and records it here; equal-rank overlaps are resolved by inserting a rank, never reordering or forbidding.

**Rank table (stage 1).** Offset is base plus rank times step, both from balance config, defaults 3 mm and 3 mm, in pipeline figure units; the runtime multiplies by `person.frame.scale` (B9); a shell may override its base (breastCloth's named response).

| Rank | Garments |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | hairBag-crown (stage 5) |
| 1 | neckBeads-neck, breastCloth-chest, loinFlap and girdleTails bands (stage 3) |
| 2 | trousers-waist, neckBeads-chest |
| 3 | waistBeads-waist, veil-face (stage 5) |
| 4 | apron band, skirtShort, skirtKnee, wrapLong-waist (stage 3) (B8) |
| 5 | shirt, robe, both togas, wrapLong-chest (stage 4) |
| 6 | hood (stage 5) |
| 7 | capes and cloaks (stage 5) |

Rigid pieces have no rank: cap, headband, headRing, topknot, turban, limbRings, babySling. Hair cap and eyes are pipeline-only. The veil is a legacy garment throughout stage 1 and keeps its cover-mask hide and push.

**Region scalars.** After `build_body` and before any cut, body.py stores `body['region'] = {'y': pos[:, 1], 'arm': W[:, arm bones].sum(1), 'leg.L': ..., 'leg.R': ...}` from the full-width `W`: `y` the rest height, `arm` the sum of the six arm bone columns (as garments.py's `Body.arm`), `leg.L` and `leg.R` the sum of thigh, shin, foot and toe per side (as `Body.leg`; kept for stage 3's leg-split skirts, unused by stage 1). These are the only inputs to region classification. They are extended by the split and never recomputed; `arm` and `leg.*` are not re-read from `W` after the split. Heights come from `landmarks(body['joints'], H)` as in garments.py, H the stature from config. The hem is knee − 0.5 (knee − ankle), knee being `shin.L` and ankle `foot.L`; the body is X-symmetric, so one height serves both legs.

**Body pre-split.** A generic split tool in body.py cuts the decimated body along the level set of a region scalar at a value c, optionally restricted to faces inside a height interval. On every crossed edge it inserts one vertex at the crossing parameter t with position, every morph delta, every region scalar and the full weight vector interpolated at t; the cut scalar is then set to c exactly, so the vertex is on its level set in the classification scalars by assignment; the weights go through `top4` and then the B1 refit from revision 3 (non-negative least squares over the four bones, sum one, over the rest pose, the design pose and the plus and minus 45 and 90 degree rotations about two axes per carried bone), stored back into the full-width `W` with four nonzero entries so export.py's `top4` is the identity on them. The skin weights are read only by skinning. No snap fallback. Each split triangle becomes two or three with no hanging vertex; `tri_uv` is interpolated per split corner. `body['map']` is extended by `surface_map` on the inserted positions against `basis_full`; morph deltas of inserted vertices are the edge interpolation of their endpoints' deltas, exact at every corner. The cuts, in order, with the whole body cut unless an interval is given:

- `y` at girdle (hip + 0.06 H); `y` at hip + 0.013 H and hip + 0.027 H (waist beads band)
- `y` at hem knee − 0.5 (knee − ankle) (trousers; one height, both legs)
- `y` at chestTop and at waist − 0.01 H (breast cloth); `y` at chestTop − 0.06 H and neck − 0.008 H (chest collar); `y` at neck + 0.002 H (neck band top)
- `arm` at 0.2 (the arm boundary) restricted to faces with `y` in [hem, neck + 0.002 H], which contains every stage-1 region; the contour is one closed loop per shoulder plus nothing elsewhere, because arm weight is zero on trunk and leg skin away from the shoulders and one on the hands that hang beside the thighs

Every body triangle gets a 32-bit region mask, one bit per stage-1 shell, set when all three vertices satisfy that shell's predicate on `body['region']`: a closed height interval on `y` and `arm` at most 0.2. Because every inserted vertex carries the level value exactly, each face lies on exactly one side of every contour. Each shell's boundary rows are the boundary edges of its region face set, chained into closed loops of body vertex indices in loop order; a height cut that also loops round a hand contributes only its trunk loop to a shell. The face region masks, each shell's boundary rows, its rank and its base offset ship in `scene.extras.villager.shells`. The clip-frame regression over every inserted vertex and every split original triangle runs in the garments step as revision 3 describes, reported per edge against 1 mm and 3 mm. Named response at 3 mm or more: the edge is listed in the garments-step report and the implementing session widens the refit pose set for that vertex's bones; the picture decides whether the error shows.

**Shell regions (stage 1).** Predicates on `body['region']`; every row carries `arm` ≤ 0.2.

| Shell | Region |
| --- | --- |
| trousers-waist | `y` in [hem, girdle], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| breastCloth-chest | `y` in [waist − 0.01 H, chestTop], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| neckBeads-chest | `y` in [chestTop − 0.06 H, neck − 0.008 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| neckBeads-neck | `y` in [neck − 0.008 H, neck + 0.002 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| waistBeads-waist | `y` in [hip + 0.013 H, hip + 0.027 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |

The trousers' region takes pelvis, seat, crotch and both legs by height, as the legacy seat tube did; the arms and the hanging hands are out by the arm scalar. The waist beads band is the trunk loop between its two height cuts alone; the hand and wrist cross-sections at the same height carry arm scalar 1 and are out.

**Shell builder (one recipe, two implementations).** Inputs: a body (positions, full uncut index, weights), the shell's face set, rank, base offset, the offset scale (1 in the pipeline, `person.frame.scale` at runtime), and its boundary rows (its own and every higher-rank shell's loops inside its region). Output: a geometry whose vertex k has a source index into the body. Positions are source plus (base + rank × step) × scale along the body's area-weighted vertex normal, recomputed from the positions it is given on the full index by `vertex_normals` in Python and the equivalent loop in TS. Each shell vertex carries its source's weights and skin indices, its source's normal as its own normal attribute, its source's `garmentPush` (from `decodeCover` with the legacy worn list, so shell and source are pushed along identical skinned vectors), and zero relative morph targets of the shell's vertex count with the composed body's target names and `morphTargetsRelative = true` (as `gltfGarment` does). No uv attribute (B3): the shell carries exactly the attribute set of the painted body part and goes through `paintSurface` with `surfaceOf` and `fibreOf` on its positions, as a legacy garment does. A rim strip, a ring of quads from the offset row down to an un-offset copy of the same body vertices, is emitted at every boundary row that is a drawn edge: the shell's own rows always, a higher-rank shell's row only when that shell is worn. The rim strip is lit with the body's normal, one offset wide (3 to 12 mm), a recorded residual. The body's boundary vertices stay in the drawn faces outside the region.

**Face cut, at assembly with the worn shell set and the legacy worn list.** A body triangle is cut if its region mask shares any bit with a worn shell, and independently by `maskedIndex` for the legacy mask. A shell triangle is cut if its source triangle's mask contains the bit of a worn shell of higher rank, or if all three of its source vertices are hidden by the worn legacy garments. Nothing is cut by a lower or equal rank.

**Data flow.**

- Pipeline: the garments step stores the five shells in `garments['meshes']` under their legacy names as `{'part': 'shell', 'faces': ..., 'rows': ..., 'rank': ..., 'base': ...}` with no positions, weights, uv or morph deltas, and `garments['meta'][name]` gains `kind: 'shell'`. Every pipeline reader that wants a shell's geometry calls the recipe on the body corner it is drawing (B4). mask.py gives a shell an empty mask entry and leaves it out of `mask['garments']`; `MK.check` skips shell parts; `S.outfits` keeps naming them; sheets.py draws them by the recipe; penetration.py routes them to the shell rows and reports their legacy baseline rows as `migrated` (B6). pipeline.py's export filter is `n.startswith('g-') and g.get('part') != 'shell'`; export.py writes no mesh for a shell and adds `extras.villager.shells`: bit names, per-face region masks, ranks, boundary rows, base offsets and the step. cachekeys cover the shell module and the region scalars.
- Runtime: `gltfPerson` is unchanged apart from reading the shell extras into `asset.shells`. `gltfFigureGeometry` partitions `layers` into shell and legacy layers before `layerOf` (B7), paints the body, decodes the legacy cover once with the legacy worn list (shell names are not in the cover order and `wornBits` skips them), builds each worn shell from the painted body's positions on the full index with offset scale `person.frame.scale` (B9), cuts body and shell faces by the rule above, sets each shell's push from the source flags, paints the shells, merges, and copies the relative flag and target names as today. `gltfLayerGeometry` returns null for a shell layer before the missing-garment log. The runtime reads the per-face masks and boundary rows from the extras and never classifies. One geometry and one draw call per figure.
- Round trip: villagerRuntimePose.test.tsx exports the composed rest body (after morph bake, hang, scale and `stylisePeaks`), `person.frame.scale`, and every worn shell's rest positions for the test outfits; gamepath.py recomputes the shells from the exported rest body with the exported scale and compares within 1e-4, then applies skinning and push to both and reads the two clearance rows of B5. A second comparison against gamepath's own drawn body with a `stylisePeaks` mirror is the detector of the full chain, as in revision 3.

**Migration of the five.** Each moves as a whole mesh in one commit; reference builders as listed in revision 3 (garments.py `build_form`). After migration the five are the regions of the table above at the ranks of the rank table; the breast cloth has no fill and follows the bust contour without bridging the cleft (recorded residual; named response: raise breastCloth's base offset by one step, and if the picture still reads as painted skin the breast cloth reverts to its legacy hull-tube builder and migrates with the trunk shells in stage 4). Bands are a rank step thick and read flat, a recorded residual. The legacy seat tube's hem at hip − 0.08 H is not needed. `finish`, `push_out`, weight transfer, `unskin` and `surface_map` are not called for these five. GARMENTS keeps every entry so `build_form` output order is unchanged; the veil branch of `build_form` is untouched.

**Legacy garments over and under the shells.**

- Over shells: shirt, robe, toga and wrapLong-chest over the breast cloth and collars; hood and veil over the collars; every outer piece over everything; shirt over trousers; loin flap, apron, girdle tails and skirts over the waist beads (rank 4 over rank 3 once the skirts are shells; until stage 3 they are legacy garments hiding by cover). The shell is hidden where its three sources are hidden by the legacy garment and pushed with its sources near openings. The remaining risk is a shell face at an opening standing outside the legacy cloth; the "legacy garment against co-worn shell" row measures it and the robe-over-trousers and flap-over-beads pictures judge it. Named response: raise that legacy garment's ease by one rank step until stage 3 or 4 migrates it.
- Under shells: neck beads under the chest collar where they overlap, waist beads over trousers (rank 3 over rank 2), with rim strips at the cut rows: the inner shell's cut edge closes to the skin with a strip from its offset to 0, the outer shell's own rim closes from its larger offset to 0 on the same body vertices, so the outer strip encloses the inner.
- Body under shells: a body vertex both hidden by a legacy garment and cut by a shell's bit is absent; a body vertex pushed by a legacy garment carries its shell with it by the shared normal and push.

**Tests.**

- Pipeline selftests: the split tool on a toy triangle (interpolation, exact level value on the cut scalar, non-negative normalised four weights, region bits unchanged by a refit that moves the weights across the threshold; map extension); the shell recipe on a toy mesh (offset along `vertex_normals` scaled by a given factor, rim strip at own and cut rows, cut rule for body and shell faces, hidden-source rule, boundary rows chained from the region face set); the trousers predicate on the real body (every face between hem and girdle with arm scalar 0 carries the bit, pelvis and crotch included); the waist-beads predicate on the real body (one connected component, exactly two boundary loops, arm scalar 0 at every corner, no face with a corner at arm ≥ 0.2 carrying the bit); the same component and arm-corner assertions for the two collars; gltfio export with shells writes five extras entries and no shell mesh; `S.outfits`, `MK.check` and `P.report` on an outfit naming all five shells run without KeyError and the baseline reports five `migrated` rows (B6).
- Garments step: the clip-frame regression over EXPORT_CLIPS at every CORNERS corner, per edge, 1 mm and 3 mm.
- penetration.py, no gate, per body corner: shell against its own source fan (band, rims apart); shell against another body part (reported with the part, no offset response); legacy garment against each co-worn shell. Legacy rows keep their baseline and must be unchanged within the report's noise.
- Vitest: shell identity on the composed body for every test outfit (position at (base + rank × step) × `person.frame.scale`, asserted on a child and an adult; normal and push copied from source; zero relative targets with the body's names; rim rows at offset zero; attribute-name set equal to the painted body part's); shell layers never reach `layerOf` and `gltfLayerGeometry` returns null for them without logging; push composition with a legacy garment over each shell; face cut completeness including hidden-source faces; bit-order agreement; the rest-space round trip with the scale; villagerFigureBody.test.ts accepting shell descriptions as a mesh for a generated pair; villagerGarmentMask.test.ts against the regenerated check json.
