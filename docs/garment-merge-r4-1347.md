# Villager garment construction — merged plan, revision 4, stage 1 (Fable 5.1)

Answers GPT-6 Astra's round-4 break check (docs/garment-check-4-1347.md, B1-B8) for stage 1 only and replaces the stage-1 part of docs/garment-merge-r3-1347.md. Stage scope is unchanged from revision 3: gate removal, before pictures, outfit enumeration and rank table, the body pre-split, the shell builder, and the migration of trousers, breastCloth, veil, neckBeads-neck, neckBeads-chest and waistBeads. Column bones stay at the start of stage 3.

Three simplifications this revision makes, each because the round-3 mechanism could not be made sound cheaply: the breast-cloth hull fill is removed (B7, B8); shells are no longer promised to fit.py as inner layers (B4); the round trip compares the recipe in rest space and keeps the posed reading as a detector (B3). One mechanism is added because it is cheap and closes a recorded stage-1 risk: a shell face whose three source vertices a worn legacy garment hides is hidden with them, which restores most of the inner mask revision 3 said was lost.

Acceptance standard, applied throughout: visibly convincing clothing at 3 m and 7 m in the port city and the village, judged in pictures on both backends; measured numbers are detectors with a named response, not a proof of zero penetration.

## 1. Round-4 objection answers

- **B1 (live morph targets)** FIXED. `bakePose` gives the body relative hand targets (villagerFigureBody.ts, `g.morphAttributes = live.length ? { position: live } : {}` and `morphTargetsRelative = true`) and `gltfGarment` gives every garment zero targets of the same count and names. The TS shell recipe now does the same as `gltfGarment`: for every target of the composed body it emits a zero attribute of the shell's vertex count carrying the target's name, and sets `morphTargetsRelative = true`. Rim vertices are included (there are no fill vertices any more). No shell reaches a hand, so zero deltas are correct, not an approximation. The vitest identity test asserts target count, names, lengths and the relative flag on every shell before the merge.
- **B2 (scalar shader push)** FIXED. Revision 3's "built from the pushed positions" was wrong: `applyGarmentMask` sets a scalar `garmentPush` attribute and `garmentPushPosition` subtracts it along `normalLocal` in the vertex shader; no position is edited. Revision 4 makes the composition exact by sharing the displacement vector, not by copying a scalar onto a different normal: every shell vertex, offset row and rim row alike, carries as its `normal` attribute the source body vertex's area-weighted normal (the same vector the offset was taken along), and its `garmentPush` is the source vertex's value from `decodeCover` with the legacy worn list. Shell and source then skin with identical weights and are pushed along identical skinned vectors, so a pushed skin vertex moves its shell vertex and its rim foot by the same vector and a neckline rim never separates from pushed skin. The cost is that a rim strip is lit with the body's normal rather than its own; the strip is one offset wide (3 to 12 mm) and this is recorded as a residual to look for in the pictures. The pipeline mirror composes the same way: mask.py's push, applied along `vertex_normals` of the full body, to body and shell alike. The vitest identity test runs on a forced outfit with a legacy garment over each shell and checks that pushed source and shell rim foot coincide after the push is applied on the CPU.
- **B3 (posed reconstruction)** FIXED. The round trip no longer recomputes shells from posed positions. villagerRuntimePose.test.tsx exports the composed rest body (after morph bake, hang, scale and `stylisePeaks`) and each worn shell's rest positions; gamepath.py runs the recipe on the exported rest body and compares within 1e-4, which is an exact test of the recipe in the one coordinate space it is defined in. The runtime deformation (skinning, push) is then applied to both in gamepath.py and the posed shell-to-body clearance is read as a detector: the interior of a shell is expected within one offset step of its nominal offset, rims and the 0.5-weight leg boundary are reported separately and not held to it, and a reading outside the band names the frame, shell and vertex. The named response is to inspect that frame in the picture; a reading under half the offset on more than one frame raises that shell's base offset by one step.
- **B4 (fit.py inner layers)** FIXED by withdrawal. The attached fit.py selects by `n.startswith('g-')` in `fit`, `settle`, `settle_once` and `smooth_cloth`, and `settle_layers` pairs from `M.masks()['garments']`; an `s-` record would be in none of them, so revision 3's "fixed inner layer" fitting would not run. Revision 4 does not change fit.py: shells are not fitted, settled, masked or paired, and stage 6 deletes fit.py anyway. Shells are stored under a new `garments['shells']` key, not in `garments['meshes']`, so no filter in fit.py, mask.py or export.py has to know about them (export.py writes every entry of `meshes`, hair and eyes included, so the `s-` prefix alone would not have kept them out of the glb). What takes the inner layer's place: the legacy garment keeps the clearance the settle gives it off the body, the shell sits at its offset off the same body, and the new penetration row "legacy garment against co-worn shell" reads the difference per frame. Named response: a shell showing through a legacy garment in the picture raises that garment's ease by one rank step, as revision 3 already stated.
- **B5 (body map after the split)** FIXED. The split tool extends `body['map']` as it extends `pos`, `morph_pos` and `W`: for the inserted vertices it calls `surface_map` on their positions against `basis_full` (the body step already runs in Blender for `decimate`), so every vertex of the split body has a nearest full-mesh triangle, barycentrics and offset. Morph deltas of inserted vertices are the edge interpolation of their endpoints' deltas, which is exact at every corner because the inserted point is the same edge point in every corner shape; the map is provenance for other consumers, not the source of the deltas. The fill, which was the only stage-1 reader of the female shape through the map, is gone (B7).
- **B6 (snap fallback and region labels)** FIXED by removing the snap. The fallback that moved an iso-crossing to an endpoint is withdrawn; a crossing vertex is always inserted on its edge, refitted where needed, and its remaining clip-frame error is reported per edge with the 1 mm and 3 mm thresholds as detectors. Every inserted vertex therefore lies on its level set, every face of the split body lies on one side of every level set, and the all-three-vertices interval rule labels faces consistently with the contour by construction. A band is never erased because both its level sets are always cut. Named response at 3 mm or more: the edge is listed in the garments-step report and the implementing session widens the refit pose set for that vertex's bones; the picture decides whether the error shows.
- **B7 (fill topology)** FIXED by removing the hull fill. The decimated body's triangles are arbitrary and hull-touch points fall inside edges, so a conforming fill would need its own cuts, shared edge vertices and a retriangulation; that is not cheap. The breast cloth is a plain offset shell of its region at rank 1 like the other five. Residual, recorded: it follows the bust contour and does not bridge the cleft. Named response if the breast-cloth picture reads as painted skin: raise breastCloth's base offset by one step (shell offsets are per-shell parameters), and if that still fails the breast cloth reverts to its legacy hull-tube builder (its GARMENTS entry is kept) and migrates with the trunk shells in stage 4.
- **B8 (collar over the fill)** FIXED. With the fill gone both pieces follow the skin and the overlap band chestTop − 0.06 H to chestTop is decided by rank: breast-cloth faces carrying the collar's bit are cut. The transition the objection asks for is the new rule that every cut row gets a rim strip, not only a shell's own boundary rows: the breast cloth's cut edge at the collar's lower level set closes to the skin with a strip from 6 mm to 0, and the collar's own bottom rim closes from 9 mm to 0 on the same body vertices, so the outer strip encloses the inner and no open edge shows. breastCloth plus neckBeads-chest co-worn is added to picture acceptance.

**Defects found in revision 3 by this merger.**

- **D6 Export filter.** Revision 3 D4 relied on `s-` names being excluded from export; export.py's mesh loop writes every entry of `garments['meshes']`. Shells now live under `garments['shells']`.
- **D7 Lost inner mask had a cheap fix.** Revision 3 recorded trousers through a robe as a stage-1 risk. A shell vertex knows its source; a shell face whose three sources a worn legacy garment hides is hidden with them, which is what the legacy inner mask did for the legacy trousers. Faces at an opening, where the body is pushed not hidden, stay drawn and are pushed with the skin (B2).

## 2. The stage-1 plan

**Gate removal.** Unchanged from revision 3: pipeline.py drops the `raise RuntimeError('penetration: ...')` and the docstring sentence; `report` keeps returning `bad` and `unchecked`. Lands first on its own.

**Before pictures.** Unchanged: one forced outfit per acceptance row, 3 m and 7 m, both backends, village and port city; the dress sheets under verification/villager-body/ are the pipeline-side before record.

**Outfit enumeration.** Unchanged: the implementing session lists every generated (form, wear) pair and co-worn combination from `appearanceFor` and records it here; equal-rank overlaps are resolved by inserting a rank, never reordering or forbidding.

**Rank table (stage 1).** Offset is base plus rank times step, both from balance config, defaults 3 mm and 3 mm; a shell may override its base (breastCloth's named response).

| Rank | Garments |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | hairBag-crown (stage 5) |
| 1 | neckBeads-neck, breastCloth-chest, loinFlap and girdleTails bands (stage 3) |
| 2 | trousers-waist, neckBeads-chest, apron band, skirtShort, skirtKnee, wrapLong-waist (stage 3) |
| 3 | waistBeads-waist, veil-face |
| 4 | shirt, robe, both togas, wrapLong-chest (stage 4) |
| 5 | hood (stage 5) |
| 6 | capes and cloaks (stage 5) |

Rigid pieces have no rank: cap, headband, headRing, topknot, turban, limbRings, babySling. Hair cap and eyes are pipeline-only.

**Body pre-split.** A generic split tool in body.py cuts the decimated body along the level set of a per-vertex scalar restricted to a vertex set. On every crossed edge it inserts one vertex with position, every morph delta and the full weight vector interpolated at the crossing parameter, then `top4`, then the B1 refit from revision 3 (non-negative least squares over the four bones, sum one, over the rest pose, the design pose and the plus and minus 45 and 90 degree rotations about two axes per carried bone). No snap fallback: the vertex stays on its edge whatever the error. Each split triangle becomes two or three with no hanging vertex. `body['map']` is extended by `surface_map` on the inserted positions. The level sets are revision 3's, heights from `landmarks` with H the stature:

- girdle (hip + 0.06 H), whole trunk, and hip + 0.013 H and hip + 0.027 H (waist beads band)
- trouser hem knee − 0.5 (knee − ankle) on each leg's vertex set (leg weight over 0.5)
- chestTop and waist − 0.01 H (breast cloth), chestTop − 0.06 H and neck − 0.008 H (chest collar), neck + 0.002 H (neck band top), neck − 0.025 H and chin + 0.055 H (veil)
- the arm boundary, level set of summed arm weight at 0.2, for the breast cloth and both bead collars

Every body triangle gets a 32-bit region mask, one bit per stage-1 shell, set when all three vertices lie in that shell's height interval and vertex set; because every inserted vertex sits on its level set this labels each face on exactly one side of every contour. The face region masks, each shell's level-set rows and its rank ship in scene.extras.villager.shells. The clip-frame regression over every inserted vertex and every split original triangle runs in the garments step as revision 3 describes, reported per edge against 1 mm and 3 mm.

**Shell builder (one recipe, two implementations).** Inputs: a body (positions, full uncut index, weights), the shell's face set, rank, base offset, and its boundary rows (its own level sets and every higher-rank shell's level set inside its region). Output: a geometry whose vertex k has a source index into the body. Positions are source plus offset along the body's area-weighted vertex normal, recomputed from the positions on the full index by `vertex_normals` in Python and the equivalent loop in TS. Each shell vertex carries its source's weights and skin indices, its source's normal as its own normal attribute, its source's `garmentPush`, zero relative morph targets matching the body's (B1), and the pipeline additionally its source's morph deltas. A rim strip, a ring of quads from the offset row down to an un-offset copy of the same body vertices, is emitted at every boundary row that is a drawn edge: the shell's own two rows always, a higher-rank shell's row only when that shell is worn and so cuts there. The body's boundary vertices stay in the drawn faces outside the region. uv is cylindrical about the hips axis on the composed body (angle round the axis, height along it), so a fibre pattern keyed by uv keeps its scale; it is not `sweep`'s column fraction. The shell passes through the same paint path as a legacy garment.

**Face cut, at assembly with the worn shell set and the legacy worn list.** A body triangle is cut if its region mask shares any bit with a worn shell, and independently by `maskedIndex` for the legacy mask. A shell triangle is cut if its source triangle's mask contains the bit of a worn shell of higher rank, or if all three of its source vertices are hidden by the worn legacy garments (D7). Nothing is cut by a lower or equal rank.

**Data flow.**

- Pipeline: the garments step runs the recipe on the drawn body for the six and stores them under `garments['shells']`, outside `meshes`, so fit.py, mask.py and export.py's mesh loop never see them. export.py writes no mesh for a shell and adds `extras.villager.shells`: bit names, per-face region masks, ranks, boundary rows, base offsets, and `kind: 'shell'` in `garments` meta for the six. cachekeys cover the shell module.
- Runtime: `gltfPerson` is unchanged. `gltfFigureGeometry` paints the body, decodes the legacy cover once with the legacy worn list, builds each worn shell from the painted body's positions on the full index, cuts body and shell faces by the rule above, sets each shell's push from the source flags, paints the shells, merges, and copies the relative flag and target names as today. One geometry and one draw call per figure.
- Round trip: villagerRuntimePose.test.tsx exports the composed rest body and every worn shell's rest positions for the test outfits; gamepath.py recomputes the shells from the exported rest body and compares within 1e-4, then poses both and reports the clearance detector of B3. A second comparison against gamepath's own drawn body with a `stylisePeaks` mirror is the detector of the full chain, as in revision 3.

**Migration of the six.** Each moves as a whole mesh in one commit; reference builders as listed in revision 3 (garments.py `build_form`). After migration: trousers the region girdle to hem on both legs at rank 2; breast cloth the region chestTop to waist − 0.01 H without arms at rank 1, no fill; veil neck − 0.025 H to chin + 0.055 H round the whole head at rank 3; chest collar chestTop − 0.06 H to neck − 0.008 H without arms at rank 2; neck beads the band neck − 0.008 H to neck + 0.002 H at rank 1; waist beads the band hip + 0.013 H to hip + 0.027 H at rank 3. Bands are a rank step thick and read flat, a recorded residual. `finish`, `push_out`, weight transfer, `unskin` and `surface_map` are not called for these six. GARMENTS keeps the six entries so `build_form` output order is unchanged.

**Legacy garments over and under the shells.**

- Over shells: shirt, robe, toga and wrapLong-chest over the breast cloth and collars; hood over the veil and collars; every outer piece over everything; shirt over trousers; loin flap, apron, girdle tails and skirts over the waist beads. The shell is hidden where its three sources are hidden by the legacy garment (D7) and pushed with its sources near openings (B2). The remaining risk is a shell face at an opening standing outside the legacy cloth; the new penetration row measures it and the robe-over-trousers and flap-over-beads pictures judge it. Named response: raise that legacy garment's ease by one rank step until stage 3 or 4 migrates it.
- Under shells: waist beads under trousers and neck beads under the veil, by rank, with rim strips at the cut rows (B8).
- Body under shells: a body vertex both hidden by a legacy garment and cut by a shell's bit is absent; a body vertex pushed by a legacy garment carries its shell with it by the shared normal and push.

**Tests.**

- Pipeline selftests: the split tool on a toy triangle (interpolation, non-negative normalised four weights, region bits, map extension); the shell recipe on a toy mesh (offset along `vertex_normals`, rim strip at own and cut rows closing onto the un-offset row, cut rule for body and shell faces, hidden-source rule).
- Garments step: the clip-frame regression over EXPORT_CLIPS at every CORNERS corner, per edge, 1 mm and 3 mm.
- penetration.py, no gate: shell against body posed (B3 band, rims reported apart); legacy garment against each co-worn shell. Legacy rows keep their baseline and must be unchanged within the report's noise.
- Vitest: shell identity on the composed body for every test outfit (position, normal copied from source, push copied from source, targets as B1, rim rows at offset zero); push composition with a legacy garment over each shell (B2); face cut completeness including hidden-source faces; bit-order agreement; the rest-space round trip; villagerFigureBody.test.ts accepting shell descriptions; villagerGarmentMask.test.ts against the regenerated check json.

**Picture acceptance (3 m and 7 m, both backends, village and port city, forced outfits).**

- trousers alone, walk and run, side-on and three-quarter, adult man and child
- trousers under shirt and under robe in walk
- breast cloth in walk and dig, front-on, adult woman at the stout and slight builds, judged specifically at the cleft
- breast cloth with the chest collar, front-on, idle and walk (B8)
- veil with and without a hood, and with a cap, idle with the head turned
- chest collar and neck beads over bare skin and under a robe neckline, idle
- waist beads under a loin flap and under an apron in walk, and over trousers in walk

Judged for holes, cloth reading as skin, beads reading as a torus, dark marks, a rim strip lit wrong (B2 residual), and a hem or neckline standing off the skin. A surviving dark mark is traced to its mesh with the debug unlit material on both backends before it is classified.

## 3. Left for later stages

- Stage 3: column bones with runtime-computed pivots, the driver with its solve, envelopes, chord target, ground bounds and verification pass, hem deltas, partial-panel grid, per-form flare and ease, hold-at-rest routing, junction contract, migration of skirtShort, skirtKnee, loinFlap, apron and girdleTails, penetration.py garment-against-garment and ground rows.
- Stage 4: trunk shells plus driven hems for shirt, robe, both togas and both wraps, and the breast cloth's cleft if the stage-1 shell fails its picture; delete `tube`, `sleeve`, `armholes`.
- Stage 5: cloaks and capes with arm clearance, hood shell, head envelopes and outfit-dependent offsets, sling, limb rings, hair bag, `finish_rigid`; delete `column_sweep`, `skirt_weights`, `OUTER`, `SKIRTS`, `RIGID_HEAD`, `RIGID_CHEST` and the per-slot layer table.
- Stage 6: delete fit.py, `finish`, `unskin`, the mask classes, the body cover export, the runtime push and hide; move calibratable values to balance config; after pictures and the full sheets.
