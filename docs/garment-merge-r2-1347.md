# Villager garment construction — merged plan, revision 2 (Fable 5.1)

Answers GPT-6 Astra's round-2 break check (docs/garment-check-2-1347.md) and the merger's audit (docs/garment-merge-r2-audit-1347.md); replaces docs/garment-merge-r1-1347.md. Next: Astra round-3 break check.

I'll answer every round-2 objection and audit defect, then write the complete revision 2 of the merged plan with the revised implementation order, then list the union changes and any further defects I can point at in the attached material.

## 1. Objection answers

- **B1** FIXED. The migration section gains a last-caller table. `skirt_weights` goes in step 5 with the cloak (`cloak` is in `SKIRTS` and `finish` calls it for every form whose lowest vertex lies under the crotch). Step 4 deletes only `tube`, `sleeve` and `armholes`.
- **B2** FIXED. Twenty-four bones in a three-level hierarchy, hips to `col.U.k` to `col.L.k`, each with a defined head and tail and a joint-delta rule (Loose garments). Both drivers write local quaternions; world rotation is parent world times local along that chain; the round trip compares world rotations of all 24.
- **B3** FIXED. Every constraint on a segment is a lower bound on one outward tilt angle in the column's radial plane, so the solve is the maximum of closed-form bounds, two passes, clamped at a maximum tilt. The junction row is exempt by construction (the pelvis envelope is clamped under the girdle ring). A verification pass over every row vertex follows the solve; what remains is measured, not re-solved.
- **B4** FIXED. The clearance target per column is sqrt(C² + (c/2)²), c the chord to each neighbour after the solve, which bounds the chord's distance to any convex obstacle given both ends clear by that target (proof in the Loose garments paragraph). Hips-blended rows are covered by the verification pass on the blended positions, and penetration.py reports what it finds with a named response.
- **B5** FIXED. Envelope radii are taken over every vertex with weight above 0.1 on the bone, not dominant vertices, with garment thickness added, and a containment test skins the body through every frame of every exported clip at every body corner and fails on any leg, pelvis or foot vertex outside its capsules.
- **B6** FIXED. All driven garments share one row schedule and one weight per (row, column), so the same affine blend maps inner and outer rows; radii and flare are per rank, so an outer rank is wider at every shared row; penetration.py measures garment-against-garment for the layered outfits regardless.
- **B7** FIXED. The ground is a lower-bound constraint on the same tilt angle for both segments, applied to every row a segment carries, in the figure's grounding frame (y = 0 as gltfPerson grounds). A pivot below ground clamps the segment horizontal and is a measured residual; blended rows are checked by the verification pass and reported.
- **B8** FIXED. Rows are the sorted union of every hem landmark plus the knee; each row's delta is the interpolation between the two landmark deltas it lies between (girdle vertex, knee joints, ankle joints), stature offsets have no delta because H is constant, and front/back hems are separate rows.
- **B9** FIXED. Hull fills are separate strips with their own vertices, weights and deltas interpolated along the ruling from the two body boundary vertices, flagged as fill faces, with the body faces under them cut by the fill's own bit.
- **B10** FIXED. The split vertex's position, deltas and full weights are interpolated, then reduced to four; a regression test skins original and pre-split bodies through every clip frame at every body corner with a 1 mm tolerance, and a failing edge gets its four weights refit by least squares to the interpolated skinned positions.
- **B11** REFUTED. Shells carry no `_COVER` and are absent from `garmentMask.garments`, so `applyGarmentMask(g.clone(), null, ...)` has nothing to apply to a shell, and the body push under a legacy garment moves body vertices away from that garment, never outward through a shell built from the unpushed body. The plan now states the order anyway: shells and the identity test take `person.geometry` before `applyGarmentMask`.
- **B12** FIXED. Waist beads are a fixed rank-3 band in every outfit; no garment hosts them. A driven garment worn under a still-legacy outer garment that reaches below the girdle is held at rest by the driver until that outer garment migrates.
- **B13** FIXED. Outfit enters only the driver and the face cut, both inside `gltfFigureGeometry`, which receives the whole `layers` list; per-layer builds and their cache stay outfit-independent.
- **B14** FIXED. A `finish_rigid` path for retained pieces (head weights, morph binding by `surface_map`, a clearance pass that pushes any vertex inside the skull, not only shallow ones), clearance verified at every body corner, and a hair-bag profile with a side-silhouette acceptance.
- **B15** FIXED. Limb rings are worn inside everything; the sling stays outside at its built standoff; both have a contact check against every worn garment in every clip.
- **B16** FIXED. One shell recipe implemented twice, with the game-path module mirroring the full drawn-body chain, and the round trip carries shell and junction positions per frame.
- **B17** FIXED. Every junction is one shared vertex row, and each adjoining piece's weight and delta rule is specified to agree there (Cloaks and capes, Head pieces).
- **B18** FIXED. Step 1 enumerates the generated pairs and co-worn combinations from appearance.ts, the equal-rank check runs over that enumeration, neck-ring beads move to rank 1 ahead of it, and rigid pieces are exempt by name.
- **Audit A2** FIXED. The self-question is gone from step 2.
- **Audit A3** FIXED. Beads are never hosted; the driver counts every worn shell band within a column's height span.
- **Audit A4** FIXED. The hood is one shell over head, neck, shoulder caps and trunk to waist plus 0.04 H; no rigid part is claimed.
- **Audit A5** FIXED. The toga shell includes the covered side's upper-arm region above chest-top.
- **Audit A6** FIXED. The left-shoulder cape mirrors the right-shoulder cape, bottom at waist minus 0.02 H, step 5.
- **Audit A7** FIXED. The BONES schema gains a computed-head kind and the heads, tails and deltas are defined.
- **Audit A8** FIXED. See B10.
- **Audit A9** FIXED. Hair cap and eyes are out of the rank table, listed as pipeline-only.
- **Audit A10** FIXED. Eyes keep `eyes()` unchanged with no clearance.

## 2. Revised merged plan

**Diagnosis.** A's four mechanisms stand as the working diagnosis: hull tubes across both legs blended between the thighs, the rest shape inflated to the union of all poses, weights transferred from the nearest body point, and a pose-independent mask that turns each cloth error into a hole. B could not see the clip, rig or pose files and rightly calls the cause of the walking-trouser report unproven. The plan keeps A's hypothesis for report (1) as a hypothesis and makes the in-game walking trouser shot the test. B's two code findings are accepted on the files B saw: the pipeline raises on penetration over tolerance, which contradicts point 1339 and goes first; and the runtime hide-bit union can delete a triangle no single garment covers, which exact face sets remove.

**Tight garments** are body shells. Trousers, the trunk of shirt and robe with upper arms, the trunk of the toga with the covered side's upper arm above chest-top, the trunk of the chest wrap without arms, the breast cloth, the hip, girdle and loin-flap bands, the neck-ring beads, the covering chest beads, the waist beads, the veil, the hood and the hair bag are copies of the drawn body's region. The body mesh is pre-split in the body step along every garment iso-line, so each region boundary is a body vertex row. A split vertex on edge (a, b) at parameter t takes position, every morph delta and the full weight vector interpolated, then `top4`. The body step runs a regression test: the original and pre-split bodies are skinned through every frame of every exported clip at every body corner, and every original vertex must lie within 1 mm of the pre-split surface. An edge that fails gets its four weights refit by least squares to the interpolated skinned positions over those frames. Each body triangle carries a region bitmask recorded at build and exported with the face sets. A shell vertex carries a source index into the body. At runtime it is rebuilt from `person.geometry` after morph bake, hang, grounding, scale and `stylisePeaks`, before `applyGarmentMask`, offset along the body vertex normal by its rank; weights are copied from the body's expanded skin attributes. Normals are area-weighted face normals as `computeVertexNormals` makes them, mirrored in the game-path module together with `stylisePeaks`. The pipeline builds shells for sheets and penetration from the same recipe on its drawn body, and the round trip carries sampled shell vertices and every junction row per frame so the two implementations are compared on the same surfaces. Hull fill is a separate construction: where a region spans a concavity (the bust under chest wrap and breast cloth), a strip of new vertices is ruled between the two body boundary curves, its weights and morph deltas interpolated along each ruling from the two boundary body vertices, its faces flagged as fill, and the body faces under the strip cut by that garment's bit. Fill is allowed only where both boundary vertices carry at least 0.9 on the chest bone. Cuffs and necklines keep the body's boundary row under a rim strip. Up to about 1 cm of ease on convex parts tapers to the base offset at the armpit, crotch and back of the knee. B's warning that this reads bra-like or painted on is a residual to judge in the picture. Bands are thin: a rank step thick at most, so a neck ring or waist beads no longer reads as a torus; recorded as a residual.

**Loose garments** hang from a driven skirt rig; B's runtime cloth solver is rejected. Everything below the girdle line, meaning both skirts, both long wraps, the hems of robe, shirt and toga, the loin-flap, apron and girdle-tail panels, and the lower panels of cloaks, is a set of columns joined to the bottom ring of the garment's shell or band. Twelve columns of two segments each are skeleton bones appended after the existing 22. skeleton.py's BONES schema gains a computed-head kind beside the MakeHuman-group kind: `col.U.k` has parent `hips`, head at the body's girdle iso-line point at ring angle k (the twelve angles of `angles(12)` about the hips axis), tail at the knee pivot; `col.L.k` has parent `col.U.k`, head at that knee pivot and tail at the ankle pivot. The knee pivot is the head's x and z at the mean y of the two shin joints; the ankle pivot likewise at the mean y of the two foot joints. body.py extends `joints` and `joint_deltas` with these rows: the head's delta is the girdle iso-line vertex's delta at that angle, the knee pivot's delta is the head's x and z delta with the mean y delta of the shin joints, the ankle pivot's the same with the foot joints. `W` and `Wfull` gain zero columns; every clip's quaternion array gains identity rows; `write_clip` writes no channel for column bones, which the driver owns. Rows are a shared schedule: the sorted union of every hem landmark (mid-thigh, knee minus 0.015 H, knee plus 0.02 H, knee plus 0.03 H, knee plus 0.05 H, hip minus 0.10 H, hip minus 0.14 H, the wrap's knee minus 0.6 of knee-to-ankle, ankle plus 0.03 H) plus the knee, so every garment's hem lies on a row every other garment also has. The junction row is the shell's bottom row with the body's weights, which is exactly the column heads' motion since they are children of hips. From the next row down a vertex is weighted to hips and its two nearest columns by angle, hips fading from 1 to 0 over the first three rows, never to a thigh or shin; the weight per (row, column) is identical for every driven garment. A row's rest radius at angle k is the trunk hull radius at that height plus a base ease plus rank times a step plus the rank's flare, flare being 0.035 for ranks 1 and 2 and 0.06 for rank 4 in the builder's units, so the loin flap, apron and girdle tails gain about 4 mm at the hem against today. Because weights are shared, inner and outer rows at one row map through the same affine blend, and an outer rank's row stays outside by the rank step times at least cos of half the tilt difference between neighbouring columns; the driver limits that difference to 60 degrees, so at least 0.87 of the step survives. A row's morph delta interpolates between the deltas of the two landmarks it lies between: girdle vertex to knee pivot, knee pivot to ankle pivot; stature offsets carry no delta because H is a constant. One stateless driver per figure per frame, after clip blending, stride warping, lean and IK, takes the worn list. Each segment has one outward tilt angle in its column's radial plane about the hips frame. Every constraint is a lower bound on that angle: clearing each of seven capsules (both thighs, both shins, pelvis, both feet) at sampled stations along the segment, the ground at every row the segment carries, and the gravity blend of the lower segment. The solve is the maximum of these bounds, upper segment then lower, then once more with the chords recomputed, clamped at a maximum tilt of 80 degrees. The clearance target per column against a capsule is sqrt(C² + (c/2)²), where C is capsule radius plus the offsets of every worn shell or band whose region crosses the segment's height span plus the largest ease among the worn driven garments, and c is the chord to the farther neighbouring column. That target bounds the chord: for any convex obstacle, if both chord ends are at least D from it, the chord's nearest point is at least sqrt(D² − c²/4) away, since the obstacle's nearest point s is at least D from each end. Capsule radii come from the drawn body: every vertex with weight above 0.1 on the bone, maximum distance from the bone line, at rest. Their adequacy is not argued but tested: a containment test skins the body through every frame of every exported clip at every body corner and fails if any leg, pelvis or foot vertex lies outside its capsules. The pelvis capsule's radius at the girdle height is clamped under the girdle ring minus the clearance, so the junction row is feasible by construction; a pivot below ground clamps the segment horizontal. After the solve the driver skins every row vertex with its actual weights and checks all capsules and the ground; it does not re-solve, and penetration.py reports the result per frame. A reported penetration is answered in this order: raise the sagitta sample count, then the base ease, then sixteen columns; none is accepted as shading. Nested panels share rotations and ranks. The driver runs identically at every quality level and both backends; a Python mirror lives in the game-path module; the round trip carries the worn list, the 24 world rotations and sampled row positions per frame. Intermediate routing: when a driven garment is worn under a legacy garment that reaches below the girdle (shirt, robe, toga, wraps, cloak before their migration), the driver holds its columns at rest, which is the rest cone the legacy fit was made against.

**Head pieces and ornaments.** The hood is one shell at rank 5 over the head minus the face opening, the neck, the shoulder caps and the trunk down to the waist plus 0.04 H iso-line, arms excluded below the caps. The veil is a shell over the lower face and the whole neck that continues about 4 cm under a robe or shirt collar and under a cloak yoke, the overlapped part cut. Whether the Tuareg heads still read as uniform dark at 3 m is a colour and material question after the rebuild. The chest beads stay an opaque covering shell; the neck-ring beads, girdle band and waist beads are thin shell bands, the waist beads at rank 3 in every outfit, inside any driven garment and counted by the driver. The hair bag is a scalp shell patch over the hair mask's back region, offset by the base offset plus a bulge profile peaking at 0.035 H at the patch centre and falling to the base at the rim; acceptance compares its side silhouette with the before picture. Cap, headband, head ring, topknot, turban and the pipeline-only hair cap keep their builders on a new `finish_rigid`: weights all head, morph binding through `surface_map` on the basis body, and a clearance pass that moves every vertex inside the skull out to the clearance whatever its depth, with clearance verified at every body corner. Eyes keep `eyes()` unchanged. A shell worn over a rigid head piece or the hair bag takes, per vertex, the larger of its rank offset and that piece's envelope at the vertex plus one step; each such piece exports a per-body-vertex envelope map for this. Limb rings are rigid tori on the forearm and shin bones, worn inside every garment and never cut. The baby sling stays rigid on spine1 at its built standoff outside the mantle. Both get a contact check against every worn garment in every clip.

**Cloaks and capes.** Both cloaks become a shoulder yoke shell plus a hanging panel with the builder's front opening kept (from the shoulder cap down for both-shoulders, below the waist for the chest cloak), the upper part a ruled surface and the lower part on the columns. The ruled panel's top row is the yoke's bottom row, shared vertices with body weights and deltas; rows below blend to chest and spine by row fraction and interpolate deltas between that row and the girdle row; the ruled panel's bottom row is the column heads' row, shared with the driven part, weighted to hips. The yoke cuts like a rank-6 shell; the panels cut nothing. The chest cape is a shoulder shell to waist minus 0.02 H with a dropped hem lip. The right-shoulder and left-shoulder capes are mirror diagonal sash shells plus a short back panel, bottom at waist minus 0.02 H as the builder sets it, no driven part.
