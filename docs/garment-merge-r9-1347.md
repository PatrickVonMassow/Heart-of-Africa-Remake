# Villager garment construction — merged plan, revision 9, stage 1 (Fable 5.1)

Answers the round-9 cross-vendor break check (docs/garment-check-9-1347.md, B1, GPT-6 Astra) for stage 1 only and replaces docs/garment-merge-r8-1347.md in full; revision 9 stands alone. Stage scope: gate removal, before pictures, outfit enumeration and rank table, the body pre-split, the shell builder, and the migration of trousers, breastCloth, neckBeads-neck, neckBeads-chest and waistBeads. The veil keeps its legacy builder until stage 5. Column bones stay at the start of stage 3.

Change this revision makes: one rim strip per seam. A rim strip is emitted only on the drawn boundary of the stacked outfit, by the shell on the higher-offset side of the seam, and it runs from that shell's offset down to the surface drawn across the seam: the skin (offset zero) or the lower shell's offset. A shell whose drawn edge meets a higher-offset shell emits nothing there. Revision 8's rule, which had the inner shell's cut edge and the outer shell's own edge each close to the skin on the same source row, is withdrawn (B1). Everything else in revision 8 stands and is embodied below.

Acceptance standard, applied throughout: visibly convincing clothing at 3 m and 7 m in the port city and the village, judged in pictures on both backends; measured numbers are detectors with a named response, not a proof of zero penetration.

## 1. Round-9 objection answer

B1 | docs/garment-merge-r8-1347.md | FIXED. Rims are emitted only on the drawn boundary of the stacked outfit, by the higher-offset shell at each seam, from its offset down to the surface drawn across the seam (skin at zero, or the lower shell at its offset); the lower shell emits no strip there, so no row carries two strips and no two faces overlap; the picture "waist beads over trousers, walk, 3 m and 7 m, both backends" judges the waist seam, and a surviving flicker or crack is traced per mesh with the debug unlit material and fixed in the rim rule, never by an offset change.

**Why the objection is right.** With default base 3 mm and step 3 mm, trousers (rank 2) stand at 9 mm and waistBeads (rank 3) at 12 mm. The beads' two rows at hip + 0.013 H and hip + 0.027 H lie inside the trousers' region [hem, girdle], so the trousers are cut there and revision 8 emitted a trousers strip from 9 mm to 0 and a beads strip from 12 mm to 0 on the same body vertices along the same body normals. Two strips from the same row along the same normals are coplanar over 0 to 9 mm. "Encloses" was wrong: nothing separates them, so they z-fight and the beads' colour fights the trousers' colour along the seam at 3 m. The same defect, which the check did not list, sits at two more stage-1 seams: the neck band (rank 1, 6 mm) and the chest collar (rank 2, 9 mm) share the row neck − 0.008 H, and the chest collar's lower row chestTop − 0.06 H lies inside the breast cloth's region [waist − 0.01 H, chestTop] (rank 1, 6 mm). Revision 8 drew two strips to the skin at each of those rows too.

**The mechanism.** The rim rule is restated on the drawn face sets after the face cut, not on the region face sets before it:

- A rim is considered only at a *drawn boundary edge* of a worn shell S: an edge of a drawn S face whose other incident body face is not a drawn S face (outside S's region, or in S's region and cut by a higher-rank worn shell).
- The *far surface* at that edge is the highest-rank worn shell whose bit is in the mask of the body face across the edge, if its drawn faces include that face; otherwise the skin at offset zero.
- If the far surface's offset is below S's offset, S emits one quad from its own offset row down to the far offset on the same two body vertices, along the body normals (a strip from S's offset to the lower shell's offset, or to zero at the skin). If the far offset equals or exceeds S's offset, S emits nothing at that edge: the shell across the seam owns the strip, or the two surfaces meet flush.

Worked out for the three seams: at the beads' rows the trousers have no drawn boundary of their own (their faces there are cut), so they emit nothing; the beads' own rows have the trousers across them at 9 mm, so the beads emit one strip from 12 mm to 9 mm. The trousers' cut edge at 9 mm and the strip's inner row coincide exactly: same source vertices, same normals, same offset, same weights, so they coincide in every pose and leave no crack. Nothing is drawn between the skin and the trousers there, because the body faces under the trousers are cut. At the collar row the neck band emits nothing (the chest collar across it stands higher) and the chest collar emits one strip from 9 mm to 6 mm. At chestTop − 0.06 H the breast cloth is cut and emits nothing; the chest collar's strip runs from 9 mm to 6 mm. The breast cloth's region row at chestTop is inside the collar's region and is not a drawn boundary when the collar is worn, so it emits nothing; without the collar it is a drawn boundary against skin and closes 6 mm to 0 as before. Every other stage-1 row (girdle, hem, waist − 0.01 H, neck + 0.002 H, the arm contours) has skin across it and closes to zero exactly as revision 8 drew it. A legacy garment over the seam hides the strip by the hidden-source rule below, unchanged.

The far surface is found per edge. In stage 1 every loop has one far surface along its whole length (the beads' loops are trunk loops entirely inside the trousers; the hand cross-sections at that height are excluded by the arm scalar), and the selftest asserts it; a loop with two different far surfaces is reported as a selftest failure in stage 1, not drawn with a step. The builder's inputs change accordingly: instead of "its own rows and every higher-rank shell's loops inside its region", a shell receives its own boundary rows, the body's face masks (the AND of corner words), and the worn set with ranks and base offsets, so both implementations compute the same far surface from the same words. Nothing is classified at runtime; the far surface is a lookup of bits the pipeline wrote.

**The picture and its named response.** "Waist beads over trousers, walk, side-on and front three-quarter, 3 m and 7 m, both backends, village and port city", judged at the waist seam for flicker, a colour fight between bead and cloth colour, and a crack between the bead band's side wall and the trousers' edge. **Gate removal.** Unchanged from revision 3: pipeline.py drops the `raise RuntimeError('penetration: ...')` and the docstring sentence; `report` keeps returning `bad` and `unchecked`. Lands first on its own.

**Before pictures.** Unchanged: one forced outfit per acceptance row, 3 m and 7 m, both backends, village and port city; the dress sheets under verification/villager-body/ are the pipeline-side before record.

**Outfit enumeration.** Unchanged: the implementing session lists every generated (form, wear) pair and co-worn combination from `appearanceFor` and records it here; equal-rank overlaps are resolved by inserting a rank, never reordering or forbidding.

**Rank table (stage 1).** Offset is base plus rank times step, both from balance config, defaults 3 mm and 3 mm, in pipeline figure units; the runtime multiplies by `person.frame.scale`; a shell may override its base (breastCloth's named response).

| Rank | Garments |

| 0 | hairBag-crown (stage 5) |
| 1 | neckBeads-neck, breastCloth-chest, loinFlap and girdleTails bands (stage 3) |
| 2 | trousers-waist, neckBeads-chest |

Rigid pieces have no rank: cap, headband, headRing, topknot, turban, limbRings, babySling. Hair cap and eyes are pipeline-only. The veil is a legacy garment throughout stage 1 and keeps its cover-mask hide and push.

**Region scalars.** After `build_body` and before any cut, body.py stores `body['region'] = {'y': pos[:, 1], 'arm': W[:, arm bones].sum(1), 'leg.L': ..., 'leg.R': ...}` from the full-width `W`: `y` the rest height, `arm` the sum of the six arm bone columns (as garments.py's `Body.arm`), `leg.L` and `leg.R` the sum of thigh, shin, foot and toe per side (kept for stage 3, unused by stage 1). These are the only inputs to region classification. They are extended by the split and never recomputed; `arm` and `leg.*` are not re-read from `W` after the split. Heights come from `landmarks(body['joints'], H)` as in garments.py, H the stature from config. The hem is knee − 0.5 (knee − ankle), knee being `shin.L` and ankle `foot.L`; the body is X-symmetric, so one height serves both legs.

**Body pre-split.** A generic split tool in body.py cuts the decimated body along the level set of a region scalar at a value c, optionally restricted to faces inside a height interval. On every crossed edge it inserts one vertex at the crossing parameter t with position, every morph delta, every region scalar and the full weight vector interpolated at t; the cut scalar is then set to c exactly, so the vertex is on its level set by assignment; the weights go through `top4` and then the refit from revision 3 (non-negative least squares over the four bones, sum one, over the rest pose, the design pose and the plus and minus 45 and 90 degree rotations about two axes per carried bone), stored back into the full-width `W` with four nonzero entries so export.py's `top4` is the identity on them. No snap fallback. Each split triangle becomes two or three with no hanging vertex; `tri_uv` is interpolated per split corner. `body['map']` is extended by `surface_map` on the inserted positions against `basis_full`; morph deltas of inserted vertices are the edge interpolation of their endpoints' deltas, exact at every corner. The cuts, in order, with the whole body cut unless an interval is given:

- `y` at girdle (hip + 0.06 H); `y` at hip + 0.013 H and hip + 0.027 H (waist beads band)
- `y` at hem knee − 0.5 (knee − ankle) (trousers; one height, both legs)
- `y` at chestTop and at waist − 0.01 H (breast cloth); `y` at chestTop − 0.06 H and neck − 0.008 H (chest collar); `y` at neck + 0.002 H (neck band top)
- `arm` at 0.2 (the arm boundary) restricted to faces with `y` in [hem, neck + 0.002 H], which contains every stage-1 region; the contour is one closed loop per shoulder and nothing elsewhere, because arm weight is zero on trunk and leg skin away from the shoulders and one on the hands that hang beside the thighs

Every body vertex gets a 32-bit region word, one bit per stage-1 shell, set when the vertex satisfies that shell's predicate on `body['region']`: a closed height interval on `y` and `arm` ≤ 0.2. A face carries a bit when all three corners carry it. Because every inserted vertex carries the level value exactly, each face lies on exactly one side of every contour, and a contour vertex (arm = 0.2) is in the region while every face across the contour has a corner above 0.2 and is out. Each shell's boundary rows are the boundary edges of its region face set, chained into closed loops of body vertex indices in loop order; a height cut that also loops round a hand contributes only its trunk loop to a shell. The per-vertex region words, each shell's bit, rank, base offset and boundary rows, and the step, ship as described under data flow. The clip-frame regression over every inserted vertex and every split original triangle runs in the garments step as revision 3 describes, reported per edge against 1 mm and 3 mm. Named response at 3 mm or more: the edge is listed in the garments-step report and the implementing session widens the refit pose set for that vertex's bones; the picture decides whether the error shows.

**Shell regions (stage 1).** Predicates on `body['region']`; every row carries `arm` ≤ 0.2.

| Shell | Region |
| --- | --- |
| trousers-waist | `y` in [hem, girdle], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| breastCloth-chest | `y` in [waist − 0.01 H, chestTop], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| neckBeads-chest | `y` in [chestTop − 0.06 H, neck − 0.008 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| neckBeads-neck | `y` in [neck − 0.008 H, neck + 0.002 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| waistBeads-waist | `y` in [hip + 0.013 H, hip + 0.027 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |

The trousers' region takes pelvis, seat, crotch and both legs by height, as the legacy seat tube did; the arms and the hanging hands are out by the arm scalar. The waist beads band is the trunk loop between its two height cuts alone; the hand and wrist cross-sections at the same height carry arm scalar 1 and are out. The three upper regions reach the shoulders or the armpits and contain corners with `arm` in (0, 0.2]; that is intended and the selftests allow it (round-8 B2). Three region pairs meet or overlap and share seams: the waist band lies inside the trousers' region; the chest collar's lower band [chestTop − 0.06 H, chestTop] lies inside the breast cloth's region; the neck band and the chest collar abut at neck − 0.008 H. Each seam is closed by exactly one rim strip under the rule below.

**Shell builder (one recipe, two implementations).** Inputs: a body (positions, full uncut index, weights, area-weighted vertex normals over the full index, per-face region masks as the AND of corner words), the shell's face set and boundary rows, its rank and base offset, the offset scale (1 in the pipeline, `person.frame.scale` at runtime), and the worn shell set with every worn shell's bit, rank and base offset. Output: a geometry whose vertex k has a source index into the body. Positions are source plus (base + rank × step) × scale along the body's vertex normal: `vertex_normals` in Python, and at runtime the normal attribute `person.geometry` already carries from `computeVertexNormals` over the full index, run last by `stylisePeaks` (D17). Each shell vertex carries its source's weights and skin indices, its source's normal as its own normal attribute, its source's `garmentPush` (from `decodeCover` with the legacy worn list, so shell and source are pushed along identical skinned vectors), and zero relative morph targets of the shell's vertex count with the composed body's target names and `morphTargetsRelative = true` (as `gltfGarment` does). No uv attribute: the shell carries exactly the attribute set of the painted body part and goes through `paintSurface` with `surfaceOf` and `fibreOf` on its positions, as a legacy garment does.

*Rim strips (B1).* After the face cut, each drawn boundary edge of the shell (an edge of a drawn face whose other incident body face is not a drawn face of this shell) is examined once. The far surface across the edge is the highest-rank worn shell whose bit is in the far body face's mask and whose drawn faces include it; otherwise the skin at offset zero. If the far surface's offset is lower than this shell's, the shell emits one quad on the edge's two body vertices from its own offset row down to the far offset, along the body normals; if equal or higher, it emits nothing at that edge. Hence a seam carries one strip, owned by the higher-offset side: beads over trousers 12 mm to 9 mm, chest collar over the breast cloth and over the neck band 9 mm to 6 mm, every row against skin from the shell's offset to 0. The strip's inner row at a covered seam coincides with the lower shell's edge vertex for vertex (same source, normal, offset and weights), so it coincides in every pose. The strip is lit with the body's normal, one offset difference wide (3 to 12 mm), a recorded residual. In stage 1 a loop has one far surface along its whole length; a loop with two is a selftest failure, not a drawn step. The body's boundary vertices stay in the drawn faces outside the region.

**Face cut, at assembly with the worn shell set and the legacy worn list.** A body triangle is cut if its region mask (the AND of its corners' words) shares any bit with a worn shell, and independently by `maskedIndex` for the legacy mask. A shell triangle is cut if its source triangle's mask contains the bit of a worn shell of higher rank, or if all three of its source vertices are hidden by the worn legacy garments; a rim quad is cut if both of its source vertices are hidden. Nothing is cut by a lower or equal rank.

**Data flow.**

- Pipeline: the garments step stores the five shells in `garments['meshes']` under their legacy names as `{'part': 'shell', 'bit': k, 'faces': ..., 'rows': ..., 'rank': ..., 'base': ...}` with no positions, weights, uv or morph deltas, and `garments['meta'][name]` gains `kind: 'shell'`; the body's per-vertex region words are stored as `body['region_bits']` beside the scalars. Every pipeline reader that wants a shell's geometry calls the recipe on the body corner it is drawing (that corner's `body['pos']` plus `morph_pos`, normals recomputed there) with the outfit's worn shell set, so its rims close to the right far surface. mask.py gives a shell an empty mask entry and leaves it out of `mask['garments']`; `MK.check` skips shell parts; `S.outfits` keeps naming them; sheets.py draws them by the recipe at the sheet's corner, cut by region bits; penetration.py routes them to the three shell rows below and reports their legacy baseline rows as `migrated` keyed on the part change. pipeline.py's export filter is the `g-` prefix alone and passes `garments` whole. export.py (round-8 B1): `export`'s garment loop tests `g.get('part') == 'shell'` first, collects the entry and `continue`s before `add_skinned_mesh` and the babySling uv read; `add_skinned_mesh` for the body takes a `region=` argument and writes `_REGION` (VEC4 unsigned shorts: bits 0–15, 16–31, 0, 0) beside `_COVER`; after the loop `meta['shells']` holds `step`, `bits` in bit order, and per shell `bit`, `rank`, `base`, `rows`. cachekeys cover the shell module, the region scalars and the region words.
- Runtime: villagerAsset.ts reads `meta.shells` into `asset.shells` (null without one); `gltfPerson` reads the body's `_region` attribute into `person.region` as it reads `_cover`. `gltfFigureGeometry` partitions `layers` into shell layers (name present in `asset.shells.garments`) and legacy layers before `layerOf`, paints the body, decodes the legacy cover once with the legacy worn list (shell names are not in the cover order and `wornBits` skips them), builds each worn shell from the painted body's positions and normals on the full index with offset scale `person.frame.scale` and the worn shell set, cuts body and shell faces by the rule above using face masks derived from `person.region`, emits each shell's rims by the far-surface rule, sets each shell's push from the source flags, paints the shells, merges, and copies the relative flag and target names as today. `gltfLayerGeometry` returns null for a shell layer before `gltfGarment` and before the DEV log, so skinnedFigure.tsx's cache warm-up neither logs nor caches a shell. The runtime never classifies: it reads words, rows, ranks and offsets. One geometry and one draw call per figure. The shell-identity vitest asserts the attribute-name set of each shell part equals the painted body part's after `tidy` and `applyGarmentMask`; if `tidy` strips `_region` or `_cover` from the body, the shell carries the same set, which is the merge precondition.
- Round trip: villagerRuntimePose.test.tsx exports the composed rest body (after morph bake, hang, scale and `stylisePeaks`) with its normal attribute, `person.frame.scale`, and every worn shell's rest positions and rim quads for the test outfits; gamepath.py recomputes the shells from the exported rest body with `vertex_normals` and the exported scale and compares within 1e-4 (which also checks D17's normal agreement and that a covered seam's rim inner row equals the lower shell's edge), then applies skinning and push to both and reads the shell rows below. A second comparison against gamepath's own drawn body with a `stylisePeaks` mirror is the detector of the full chain, as in revision 3.

**Penetration rows for shells (penetration.py, no gate, per body corner, per EXPORT_CLIPS frame).**

- Row one, "shell against its own source": the posed distance from each shell vertex to the fan of its source vertex's incident body triangles. With identical weights the posed shell point is the posed source plus offset times the skinned normal blend, so the reading is at most the offset and drops only where the blended normals shrink it; the band is [half the offset, one step over the offset], rims reported apart.
