# Villager garment construction — merged plan, revision 8, stage 1 (Fable 5.1)

Answers the round-8 cross-vendor break check (docs/garment-check-8-1347.md, B1–B3, GPT-6 Astra) for stage 1 only and replaces docs/garment-merge-r7-1347.md in full; revision 8 stands alone. Stage scope: gate removal, before pictures, outfit enumeration and rank table, the body pre-split, the shell builder, and the migration of trousers, breastCloth, neckBeads-neck, neckBeads-chest and waistBeads. The veil keeps its legacy builder until stage 5. Column bones stay at the start of stage 3.

Changes this revision makes: the shell recipes stay in `garments['meshes']` through pipeline.py's export filter and export.py itself skips mesh emission for a `shell` part and writes the recipe to the scene extras, with the per-vertex region bits shipped as a body attribute in the `_COVER` style (B1); the arm-corner selftests distinguish the contour value 0.2, which the builder assigns and the predicate keeps, from arm > 0.2, which no region face may touch, and the all-zero assertion is kept only where the builder's regions lie below the shoulders (B2); the "shell against another body part" row excludes only the source vertex's local surface neighbourhood, so an opposite-leg penetration under the trousers is reported with its frame and its bone (B3).

Acceptance standard, applied throughout: visibly convincing clothing at 3 m and 7 m in the port city and the village, judged in pictures on both backends; measured numbers are detectors with a named response, not a proof of zero penetration.

## 1. Round-8 objection answers

- **B1 (shell recipes never reach export.py)** FIXED. The objection is right against both the attached export.py and revision 7's own text. export.py's `export` iterates `garments['meshes']` whole and calls `add_skinned_mesh` on every entry, reading `g['pos']`, `g['tris']`, `g['W']` and `g['morph_pos']` (and `g['uv']` for a babySling); it writes `meta['garments'] = garments.get('meta', {})` and nothing else about garments into `scene.extras.villager`. Revision 7 put the shell recipe only in `meshes`, gave `meta` only `kind: 'shell'`, and had pipeline.py filter shells out of `meshes` before export, so export.py could see neither faces, rows, ranks nor offsets; had the filter been left off, `add_skinned_mesh` would have raised KeyError on `g['pos']`. Revision 8 keeps the recipe in `meshes` through the export boundary and skips only mesh emission, in export.py itself: pipeline.py's export filter is the `g-` prefix alone, as today; `export` tests `g.get('part') == 'shell'` first in its loop and, for a shell, appends the entry to a local `shells` dict and `continue`s before `add_skinned_mesh` and before the babySling uv read. After the loop it writes `meta['shells'] = {'step': step, 'bits': [names in bit order], 'garments': {name: {'bit': k, 'rank': r, 'base': b, 'rows': [[vertex indices per loop], ...]}}}`. The per-vertex region bits do not go into the JSON: `add_skinned_mesh` for the body gets `extra_attrs` extended by a `_REGION` VEC4 of unsigned shorts (bits 0–15, bits 16–31, two zero shorts), written exactly as `_COVER` is, through a new `region=` argument beside `cover=`. The runtime derives a face's mask as the AND of its three corners' bits, which is the pipeline's own definition of the face set (all three vertices satisfy the predicate), so nothing is lost and nothing is classified at runtime. villagerAsset.ts gains `shells` on `Meta` and on `VillagerAsset`, read as `meta.shells ?? null`; `gltfPerson` reads the body's `_region` attribute as it reads `_cover` (GLTFLoader lower-cases it) into `person.region: Uint16Array | null`, before `tidy` can touch the painted clone. The gltfio selftest asserts: five entries under `extras.villager.shells.garments`, a `_REGION` accessor on the body with the body's vertex count, and no mesh named after a shell. `S.outfits`, `MK.check`, sheets.py and penetration.py keep reading the recipe from `meshes` as revision 7 described; mask.py keeps giving a shell no cover bits and leaving it out of `mask['garments']`, so `mask['inner'].get(name)` is never reached for a shell because export.py skips it first.
- **B2 (collar selftests reject the builder's contour)** FIXED. The split inserts every contour vertex with `arm` set to 0.2 exactly, and the predicate `arm` ≤ 0.2 keeps it, so a region face at the arm boundary has corners at exactly 0.2 by construction, and a region that spans the shoulders (neckBeads-chest from chestTop − 0.06 H up, and the breast cloth near the armpit, and possibly neckBeads-neck at the base of the neck) has interior corners with `arm` strictly between 0 and 0.2. Revision 7's "no face with a corner at arm ≥ 0.2" and the inherited "arm scalar 0 at every corner" both fail on a correct build. Revision 8's assertions, per region: no face carrying the bit has a corner with `arm` > 0.2; every corner with `arm` == 0.2 lies on one of the region's boundary loops; the region is one connected component (the collars and the breast cloth) with exactly two boundary loops for the two collars and the waist band. The all-zero assertion is kept for the waist beads band and the trousers only, which is what the builder needs there: both lie below the girdle, where arm weight is zero on trunk and leg skin, and the hands beside the thighs carry 1 and are cut away by the 0.2 contour. The waist band additionally keeps "no corner at `arm` > 0" because its loops are the two height cuts alone and no arm contour should enter it.
- **B3 (inner-thigh contacts suppressed by the region test)** FIXED. Revision 7's row two required the nearest body triangle to lie outside the shell's region face set; both legs are in the trousers' region, so a left-thigh shell vertex pushed into the right thigh in the walk's crossing phase was in-region and silent, and row one measures only the distance to the own-source fan, which stays at the offset. Revision 8 defines row two by locality, not by region: the excluded set for shell vertex k is the two-ring of its source vertex on the full uncut body index (the source, its neighbours, and their neighbours), and the test is against every other body triangle. A reading is reported when the nearest such triangle is at most one offset away and the shell point lies on its inner side (negative signed distance along the triangle's area normal), confirmed by a parity ray against the posed body so a concave fan at the crotch does not read as a hit. The row reports per frame the shell, the source's dominant bone, the hit triangle's dominant bone and the depth, so an opposite-leg penetration appears as thigh.L against thigh.R with a frame name. Its named response is unchanged and never an offset change: the frame goes to picture inspection; if it reads at 3 m or 7 m the fix is a contact in the clip or the stage-3 leg split, recorded as a residual with the frame. A hung arm against the lateral chest and a hand beside the thigh report through the same rule.

**Defects found in revision 7 by this merger**, against the attached code.

- **D15** export.py's garment loop calls `add_skinned_mesh` on every `meshes` entry and reads `g['pos']`; a recipe-only entry raises KeyError, and the shell extras were never written. Fixed by B1.
- **D16** villagerAsset.ts's `Meta` and `VillagerAsset` carry no shell field and `gltfPerson` reads only `_COVER`; revision 7 named `asset.shells` without saying where it comes from. Fixed by B1.
- **D17** Revision 7's recipe recomputes vertex normals in a TS loop; `person.geometry` already carries area-weighted normals from `computeVertexNormals` over the full index, run last by `stylisePeaks`, and `vertex_normals` in Python is the same area-weighted sum. The runtime recipe reads the body's normal attribute and the round trip asserts the two agree within 1e-4, which removes one implementation of the same thing.

Revision 7's answers to round-7 B1–B9 stand except where B1 and B2 above amend B6 and the selftests; the amended text is embodied below.

## 2. The stage-1 plan

**Gate removal.** Unchanged from revision 3: pipeline.py drops the `raise RuntimeError('penetration: ...')` and the docstring sentence; `report` keeps returning `bad` and `unchecked`. Lands first on its own.

**Before pictures.** Unchanged: one forced outfit per acceptance row, 3 m and 7 m, both backends, village and port city; the dress sheets under verification/villager-body/ are the pipeline-side before record.

**Outfit enumeration.** Unchanged: the implementing session lists every generated (form, wear) pair and co-worn combination from `appearanceFor` and records it here; equal-rank overlaps are resolved by inserting a rank, never reordering or forbidding.

**Rank table (stage 1).** Offset is base plus rank times step, both from balance config, defaults 3 mm and 3 mm, in pipeline figure units; the runtime multiplies by `person.frame.scale`; a shell may override its base (breastCloth's named response).

| Rank | Garments |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | hairBag-crown (stage 5) |
| 1 | neckBeads-neck, breastCloth-chest, loinFlap and girdleTails bands (stage 3) |
| 2 | trousers-waist, neckBeads-chest |
| 3 | waistBeads-waist, veil-face (stage 5) |
| 4 | apron band, skirtShort, skirtKnee, wrapLong-waist (stage 3) |
| 5 | shirt, robe, both togas, wrapLong-chest (stage 4) |
| 6 | hood (stage 5) |
| 7 | capes and cloaks (stage 5) |

Rigid pieces have no rank: cap, headband, headRing, topknot, turban, limbRings, babySling. Hair cap and eyes are pipeline-only. The veil is a legacy garment throughout stage 1 and keeps its cover-mask hide and push.

**Region scalars.** After `build_body` and before any cut, body.py stores `body['region'] = {'y': pos[:, 1], 'arm': W[:, arm bones].sum(1), 'leg.L': ..., 'leg.R': ...}` from the full-width `W`: `y` the rest height, `arm` the sum of the six arm bone columns (as garments.py's `Body.arm`), `leg.L` and `leg.R` the sum of thigh, shin, foot and toe per side (kept for stage 3, unused by stage 1). These are the only inputs to region classification. They are extended by the split and never recomputed; `arm` and `leg.*` are not re-read from `W` after the split. Heights come from `landmarks(body['joints'], H)` as in garments.py, H the stature from config. The hem is knee − 0.5 (knee − ankle), knee being `shin.L` and ankle `foot.L`; the body is X-symmetric, so one height serves both legs.

**Body pre-split.** A generic split tool in body.py cuts the decimated body along the level set of a region scalar at a value c, optionally restricted to faces inside a height interval. On every crossed edge it inserts one vertex at the crossing parameter t with position, every morph delta, every region scalar and the full weight vector interpolated at t; the cut scalar is then set to c exactly, so the vertex is on its level set by assignment; the weights go through `top4` and then the refit from revision 3 (non-negative least squares over the four bones, sum one, over the rest pose, the design pose and the plus and minus 45 and 90 degree rotations about two axes per carried bone), stored back into the full-width `W` with four nonzero entries so export.py's `top4` is the identity on them. No snap fallback. Each split triangle becomes two or three with no hanging vertex; `tri_uv` is interpolated per split corner. `body['map']` is extended by `surface_map` on the inserted positions against `basis_full`; morph deltas of inserted vertices are the edge interpolation of their endpoints' deltas, exact at every corner. The cuts, in order, with the whole body cut unless an interval is given:

- `y` at girdle (hip + 0.06 H); `y` at hip + 0.013 H and hip + 0.027 H (waist beads band)
- `y` at hem knee − 0.5 (knee − ankle) (trousers; one height, both legs)
- `y` at chestTop and at waist − 0.01 H (breast cloth); `y` at chestTop − 0.06 H and neck − 0.008 H (chest collar); `y` at neck + 0.002 H (neck band top)
- `arm` at 0.2 (the arm boundary) restricted to faces with `y` in [hem, neck + 0.002 H], which contains every stage-1 region; the contour is one closed loop per shoulder and nothing elsewhere, because arm weight is zero on trunk and leg skin away from the shoulders and one on the hands that hang beside the thighs

Every body vertex gets a 32-bit region word, one bit per stage-1 shell, set when the vertex satisfies that shell's predicate on `body['region']`: a closed height interval on `y` and `arm` ≤ 0.2. A face carries a bit when all three corners carry it. Because every inserted vertex carries the level value exactly, each face lies on exactly one side of every contour, and a contour vertex (arm = 0.2) is in the region while every face across the contour has a corner above 0.2 and is out. Each shell's boundary rows are the boundary edges of its region face set, chained into closed loops of body vertex indices in loop order; a height cut that also loops round a hand contributes only its trunk loop to a shell. The per-vertex region words, each shell's bit, rank, base offset and boundary rows, and the step, ship as described under data flow. The clip-frame regression over every inserted vertex and every split original triangle runs in the garments step as revision 3 describes, reported per edge against 1 mm and 3 mm. Named response at 3 mm or more: the edge is listed in the garments-step report and the implementing session widens the refit pose set for that vertex's bones; the picture decides whether the error shows.

**Shell regions (stage 1).** Predicates on `body['region']`; every row carries `arm` ≤ 0.2.

| Shell | Region |
| --- | --- |
| trousers-waist | `y` in [hem, girdle], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| breastCloth-chest | `y` in [waist − 0.01 H, chestTop], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| neckBeads-chest | `y` in [chestTop − 0.06 H, neck − 0.008 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| neckBeads-neck | `y` in [neck − 0.008 H, neck + 0.002 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |
| waistBeads-waist | `y` in [hip + 0.013 H, hip + 0.027 H], `arm` ≤ 0.2 |

The trousers' region takes pelvis, seat, crotch and both legs by height, as the legacy seat tube did; the arms and the hanging hands are out by the arm scalar. The waist beads band is the trunk loop between its two height cuts alone; the hand and wrist cross-sections at the same height carry arm scalar 1 and are out. The three upper regions reach the shoulders or the armpits and contain corners with `arm` in (0, 0.2]; that is intended and the selftests allow it (B2).

**Shell builder (one recipe, two implementations).** Inputs: a body (positions, full uncut index, weights, area-weighted vertex normals over the full index), the shell's face set, rank, base offset, the offset scale (1 in the pipeline, `person.frame.scale` at runtime), and its boundary rows (its own and every higher-rank shell's loops inside its region). Output: a geometry whose vertex k has a source index into the body. Positions are source plus (base + rank × step) × scale along the body's vertex normal: `vertex_normals` in Python, and at runtime the normal attribute `person.geometry` already carries from `computeVertexNormals` over the full index, run last by `stylisePeaks` (D17). Each shell vertex carries its source's weights and skin indices, its source's normal as its own normal attribute, its source's `garmentPush` (from `decodeCover` with the legacy worn list, so shell and source are pushed along identical skinned vectors), and zero relative morph targets of the shell's vertex count with the composed body's target names and `morphTargetsRelative = true` (as `gltfGarment` does). No uv attribute: the shell carries exactly the attribute set of the painted body part and goes through `paintSurface` with `surfaceOf` and `fibreOf` on its positions, as a legacy garment does. A rim strip, a ring of quads from the offset row down to an un-offset copy of the same body vertices, is emitted at every boundary row that is a drawn edge: the shell's own rows always, a higher-rank shell's row only when that shell is worn. The rim strip is lit with the body's normal, one offset wide (3 to 12 mm), a recorded residual. The body's boundary vertices stay in the drawn faces outside the region.

**Face cut, at assembly with the worn shell set and the legacy worn list.** A body triangle is cut if its region mask (the AND of its corners' words) shares any bit with a worn shell, and independently by `maskedIndex` for the legacy mask. A shell triangle is cut if its source triangle's mask contains the bit of a worn shell of higher rank, or if all three of its source vertices are hidden by the worn legacy garments. Nothing is cut by a lower or equal rank.

**Data flow.**

- Pipeline: the garments step stores the five shells in `garments['meshes']` under their legacy names as `{'part': 'shell', 'bit': k, 'faces': ..., 'rows': ..., 'rank': ..., 'base': ...}` with no positions, weights, uv or morph deltas, and `garments['meta'][name]` gains `kind: 'shell'`; the body's per-vertex region words are stored as `body['region_bits']` beside the scalars. Every pipeline reader that wants a shell's geometry calls the recipe on the body corner it is drawing (that corner's `body['pos']` plus `morph_pos`, normals recomputed there). mask.py gives a shell an empty mask entry and leaves it out of `mask['garments']`; `MK.check` skips shell parts; `S.outfits` keeps naming them; sheets.py draws them by the recipe at the sheet's corner, cut by region bits; penetration.py routes them to the three shell rows below and reports their legacy baseline rows as `migrated` keyed on the part change. pipeline.py's export filter is the `g-` prefix alone and passes `garments` whole. export.py (B1): `export`'s garment loop tests `g.get('part') == 'shell'` first, collects the entry and `continue`s before `add_skinned_mesh` and the babySling uv read; `add_skinned_mesh` for the body takes a `region=` argument and writes `_REGION` (VEC4 unsigned shorts: bits 0–15, 16–31, 0, 0) beside `_COVER`; after the loop `meta['shells']` holds `step`, `bits` in bit order, and per shell `bit`, `rank`, `base`, `rows`. cachekeys cover the shell module, the region scalars and the region words.
- Runtime: villagerAsset.ts reads `meta.shells` into `asset.shells` (null without one); `gltfPerson` reads the body's `_region` attribute into `person.region` as it reads `_cover`. `gltfFigureGeometry` partitions `layers` into shell layers (name present in `asset.shells.garments`) and legacy layers before `layerOf`, paints the body, decodes the legacy cover once with the legacy worn list (shell names are not in the cover order and `wornBits` skips them), builds each worn shell from the painted body's positions and normals on the full index with offset scale `person.frame.scale`, cuts body and shell faces by the rule above using face masks derived from `person.region`, sets each shell's push from the source flags, paints the shells, merges, and copies the relative flag and target names as today. `gltfLayerGeometry` returns null for a shell layer before `gltfGarment` and before the DEV log, so skinnedFigure.tsx's cache warm-up neither logs nor caches a shell. The runtime never classifies: it reads words, rows, ranks and offsets. One geometry and one draw call per figure. The shell-identity vitest asserts the attribute-name set of each shell part equals the painted body part's after `tidy` and `applyGarmentMask`; if `tidy` strips `_region` or `_cover` from the body, the shell carries the same set, which is the merge precondition.
- Round trip: villagerRuntimePose.test.tsx exports the composed rest body (after morph bake, hang, scale and `stylisePeaks`) with its normal attribute, `person.frame.scale`, and every worn shell's rest positions for the test outfits; gamepath.py recomputes the shells from the exported rest body with `vertex_normals` and the exported scale and compares within 1e-4 (which also checks D17's normal agreement), then applies skinning and push to both and reads the shell rows below. A second comparison against gamepath's own drawn body with a `stylisePeaks` mirror is the detector of the full chain, as in revision 3.

**Penetration rows for shells (penetration.py, no gate, per body corner, per EXPORT_CLIPS frame).**

- Row one, "shell against its own source": the posed distance from each shell vertex to the fan of its source vertex's incident body triangles. With identical weights the posed shell point is the posed source plus offset times the skinned normal blend, so the reading is at most the offset and drops only where the blended normals shrink it; the band is [half the offset, one step over the offset], rims reported apart. Named response: a reading under half the offset on more than one frame raises that shell's base offset by one step.
- Row two, "shell against another body part" (B3): for shell vertex k, every body triangle with no corner in the two-ring of k's source on the full uncut index; reported when the nearest such triangle is within one offset and the shell point is on its inner side by the signed distance along the triangle's area normal, confirmed by a parity ray against the posed body. Reported per frame with the shell, the source's dominant bone, the hit triangle's dominant bone and the depth, so the trousers' inner thighs read as thigh.L against thigh.R, the hung upper arm against the chest, a hand against the thigh. Named response: never an offset change; the frame is inspected in the picture at 3 m and 7 m; if it reads, the fix is a contact in the clip or the stage-3/4 cut, recorded as a residual naming the frame.
- Row three, "legacy garment against co-worn shell": a shell face at an opening standing outside the legacy cloth. Named response: raise that legacy garment's ease by one rank step until stage 3 or 4 migrates it.

Legacy rows keep their baseline and must be unchanged within the report's noise; the baseline comparison reports the five migrated legacy rows as `migrated`, not `unchecked`.

**Migration of the five.** Each moves as a whole mesh in one commit; reference builders as listed in revision 3 (garments.py `build_form`).
