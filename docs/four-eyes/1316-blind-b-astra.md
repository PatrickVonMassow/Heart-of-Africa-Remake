Villager motion: research, measured diagnosis, and ordered concept

Research date: 12 October 2026. Model: GPT-6 (Codex). This is research and design only. No implementation, source edits, asset export, or commit is proposed as completed. The recommended proof-of-concept is a small, well-authored clip set with one coherent pose pipeline and corrective contacts. A larger skeleton or motion-matching system is not the first remedy.

**Research: what natural movement communicates**

Human movement reads through support, balance, intention, and timing. A foot visibly carrying weight must stay attached to its ground contact while the pelvis moves over it. The body prepares before an effort, different segments accelerate at different times, and motion settles after the task. Perfect bilateral symmetry, constant-speed limb rotations, instantaneous changes of direction, and permanently extended elbows remove those cues. Small variation helps only after contacts and timing work; adding noise to a broken gait makes it less believable.

A useful walking reference is approximately 60% stance and 40% swing per foot, with two double-support intervals. This is a reference for ordinary adult walking, not an invariant for every speed, age, or style. Read the cycle as initial heel contact, loading response with a yielding knee, mid-stance over the planted foot, heel rise, forefoot/toe push-off, swing with knee flexion and toe clearance, then controlled extension for the next contact. The ankle rolls; the foot is not a rigid block pinned flat throughout stance. Pelvis rotation and lateral weight transfer coordinate with counter-rotation of the thorax and opposite arm swing. The head is comparatively stable without being frozen. Cadence, stride length, knee excursion and double support vary with speed. Child proportions, elder posture and loads need their own calibration, not just a playback-rate multiplier. [Gait phases and age-related gait characteristics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5318488/).

Running requires a different pattern, rather than a faster walk: shorter support, greater knee flexion, more vigorous bent-elbow arm swing, impact absorption and elastic recovery. Typical aerial running has flight intervals; slow grounded running also exists, so “both feet airborne” is not the sole test of a valid run. A run must retain meaningful loading and propulsion rather than spend almost the entire cycle hovering. Foot-strike style depends on pace and performer; forcing a heel strike onto every run is inappropriate. Acceleration needs a forward whole-body inclination and deceleration needs braking steps. [Running mechanics and grounded running](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8323463/).

A turn should show attention shifting toward the intended direction, followed by reorientation of the torso and pelvis and a change in foot placement. Head/trunk anticipation has been measured; it is not simply whole-character yaw smoothing. In-place turns generally need a support transfer and one or more steps, with limited rotation around a real support point. Curved travel has unequal inside/outside paths and step placement, rather than two identical forward strides rotated under the body. Pivot choices vary with angle and speed; the game should use a small set of believable step-turns rather than simulate every human strategy. [Anticipatory turning study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28948937/), [turning kinematics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4155102/).

For actions, the following are animation design interpretations, informed by research on coordinated reaching rather than claims of one universal human pose. A dig needs a stable stance, blade placement, loading/push, levering, lift, disposal and recovery; resistance should affect the whole body and the tool should carry both hands. Picking up needs gaze and preparation, a hip/knee bend or kneel appropriate to height, hand preshaping, actual contact, closure, then a lift with the object. Pointing needs a readable line of attention from face through hand, a softly extended elbow, suitable wrist orientation and a distinct finger shape. Reaching recruits the shoulder girdle and then the trunk as required; beyond safe reach the character should step instead of stretching an arm or tipping indefinitely. Carrying needs a consistent grip, load support and modest balancing adjustments; a head load benefits from a steadier head and one supporting hand. These choices should retain the game's clear action meanings. [Arm–trunk pointing coordination](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8983989/), [whole-body reaching and equilibrium](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10550511/).

For tag, the chaser should still read as running: preserve lower-body propulsion and the free arm's counterbalance, prepare one hand, reach toward the quarry as distance closes, and recover after a hit or miss. A permanently two-armed forward pose reads more like a stiff grab than pursuit. The caught child should brake and settle before standing dejected: feet support an almost upright body, shoulders relax forward/down, upper back rounds slightly, chin lowers independently, and weight may rest more on one leg. Avoid a deep waist bow or a crouch; those convey another action. The posture can hold with restrained breathing or a small weight shift, without wobbling its ground contacts.

**Research: real-time techniques and rig standards**

Clip playback supplies correlated motion that is hard to reconstruct from unrelated sine waves. An in-place clip can follow gameplay-authoritative displacement if phase advances from actual distance, speed scaling remains bounded, and support events survive blending. Root-motion clips are also useful offline for measuring displacement and turn angle; adopting them need not give animation control over collision or navigation. Position blending and quaternion interpolation produce continuity, but do not themselves preserve a planted foot or a hand on a prop. [Code-driven versus animation-driven displacement and foot locking](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement).

Use masked layers to reserve selected joints for an action and additive deltas for small posture, gaze, breathing and load adjustments. Additive rotations must be computed relative to a known reference pose, applied in a defined space and order, and limited so the combined result remains plausible. Independent Euler offsets are not interchangeable with rotation deltas. Use a short eased transition initially; introduce velocity-preserving inertialization only if interrupted actions still produce visible velocity discontinuities. Three.js already provides clip actions, weights, cross-fades, time scaling and additive blending; this project also already has a pure pose sampler. There is no need to add an animation package or replace its sampler solely to obtain these ideas. [Three.js AnimationAction](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/AnimationAction.html), [practical springs and damping](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call).

Corrective IK is a final constraint pass over a plausible pose, not a replacement for one. Two-bone leg/arm IK with stable pole directions is sufficient here. Feet need contact state, a terrain-relative sole frame, and bounded pelvis adjustment when two contacts disagree. Hands need object-local grip frames and a wrist orientation, not just a point. Shoulder participation and small spine distribution keep wrists reachable without elbow locking. A look-at chain should share a limited turn across chest, neck and head and release smoothly; the pelvis follows only when a step or turn is appropriate. Three has an optional CCD solver, but the existing analytic two-bone solvers are a simpler match to this rig. [Three.js CCDIKSolver](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/CCDIKSolver.html).

Motion matching selects a frame using current pose/velocity and intended future trajectory, then blends or inertializes into that continuation. It needs broad, consistent coverage of starts, stops and turns; nine clips cannot supply missing motion through search. For a later experiment, an offline feature table of foot positions/velocities, root velocity and short future trajectory samples could be searched in plain TypeScript, with continuing-frame bias, contact compatibility, minimum dwell time and the same final IK. This is technically feasible for a single-player prototype, but it is deferred, not an additional implementation item below. First compare the simple clip/state solution at the same camera and frame budget. Epic's pose-search documentation describes the relevant features and cost tradeoffs. Do not import its sample animations under an assumed CC0 licence. [Motion matching and feature selection](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine).

There is no universal “natural motion” bone count. Count deform joints separately from root, IK helpers, end markers, facial bones and corrective joints. Useful comparisons are:

**Measured diagnosis: deficits and their causes**

The existing pipeline is a useful foundation: pinned inputs are checked before use (scripts/villager/build.mjs:56–80), body morphs also move joints, tool clips solve both arms offline, and gamepath.py reproduces the two-stage hang/skin path for garment work. Blender is an offline asset tool; the game uses GLTFLoader (src/render/villagerAsset.ts:13–14). The problems below are specific limitations of that implementation, not missing-animation-system assumptions.

The rig's most immediate hand deficit is a disconnected feature. The GLB has grip.L/grip.R morphs and dig metadata requests both at 1 (scripts/villager/body.py:24, :301–308; toolclips.py:359–361). bakeMorphs preserves them (src/render/villagerBody.ts:55–82), but the next hang bake deletes all morph attributes (src/render/villagerFigureBody.ts:112–113). gltfClipLayers.ts:127–180 never applies clip.grip. Thus the live figure cannot close the hands through those targets. Finger bones were deliberately collapsed at skeleton.py:61–62, and only grip shapes exist; there is no independent pointing index. This is a stronger diagnosis than “21 bones are too few.” The existing rig can turn its head and shoulders, but much of that capability is not being used.

Locomotion quality is limited by both the selected source and its processing. walk and sprint natural speeds differ by approximately 7.21×. The runtime blends them across a threshold of 1.8 with half-band 0.35, and clamps stride scale to 0.75–1.35 (balance.ts:2672–2691). clips.py:264–283 spreads a loop-end rotation error across the entire cycle; its own comment identifies a roughly one-radian sprint shin seam. :286–305 smooths the loop, and :343–346 performs two sprint smoothing passes before globally grounding the run. Global grounding correctly avoids flattening genuine flight, but one low sample can leave other supports elevated. The measured contact windows show that the current result requires examination. There is no moderate run between the walk and sprint, and no authored acceleration, braking or turn transition (export.py:167).

The final pose combines two different gaits. skinnedFigure.tsx:336–356 first creates the procedural walk; :452–453 uses its hip/chest counter-yaw. gltfClipLayers.ts:102–105 and :147–155 then replaces hips/legs and free arms from the clip but excludes spine, chest, neck and head. Consequently the clip's trunk rhythm is discarded and procedural counter-rotation can refer to a different phase than the newly applied pelvis. Imported idle/kneel tracks are not used as the ordinary full-body runtime base; the runtime retains procedural kneelBlend instead (:340–344), despite the offline sheets showing kneel clips. This can make a good-looking source sheet an inaccurate preview of the game.

Contact correction is not final. skinnedFigure.tsx:466–490 solves a virtual hand target first; :508 then changes the pelvis/arms with clip layers. Within gltfClipLayers.ts:148–155, foot IK is calculated for a full gait pose and then blended into procedural legs using walking weight. Later dig blending modifies the whole body (:174–179). There is no final universal contact pass after all these changes. Residual contact error during transitions or carrying therefore has a structural cause; making the individual IK solver more precise does not fix subsequent parent-transform changes.

The foot solver also permits the very failure the task forbids. Held contacts only store X/Z (villagerClipPose.ts:45–47), and correction is derived from a height threshold rather than exported support events (:185–205). :224–229 deliberately moves held anchors when the correction exceeds plantRelease. This may keep the leg reachable, but it is still sliding. :241–244 preserves the clip's foot orientation rather than a terrain contact frame. GroundBody has no height/normal (:35–40). PlaceLife.tsx:1115 and :3185 place the character at terrain height sampled at its root; that does not conform two feet to different terrain heights. The measured blend-band sink and slide are concrete failures; slopes and final-composition errors are identified coverage gaps, not claimed browser measurements.

Turning already has some smoothing: children draw c.facing, not raw heading (PlaceLife.tsx:1122–1125). The deficit is the foot/body response. figureWalk.ts:442–447 converts sideways travel and absolute yaw change into forward-equivalent walked distance. At turnStep=0.4 (balance.ts:2529), a stationary quarter turn contributes 0.6283 figure units; even a stationary rapid turn can therefore look like forward walking or running in place. This same value drives clip phase and speed (skinnedFigure.tsx:563–564). There is no separate support-foot pivot state, signed inside/outside step plan or turn clip in the exported set. The turning probe quantified the resulting contact error.

Gaze and lean are constrained by a primitive-era interface. FigurePose contains two shoulder-angle triplets, lean and turn, but no neck/head aim, wrist target or elbow intent (gesture.ts:108–130). skinnedFigure.tsx:385–387 concentrates virtual trunk rotation at spine and adds elder stoop at chest/neck; :452–453 repeats the pattern after posing legs. unsquashHead resets head.quaternion even when squash is 1 (figureRig.ts:140–155), and is called after the clip layer and again at the frame end (skinnedFigure.tsx:513, :593). This erases an authored local head rotation, including the dig's authored head pitch. More bones alone would not fix that overwrite.

Digging has a coherent offline six-key tool path, two-hand IK, fixed ankle support and distributed bend (toolclips.py:199–210, :300–324); those strengths should be kept. Its limitations are the same periodic adult-authored solve on every body, no runtime second-hand correction to the final shaft, and unrelated action clocks. DIG_PERIOD is 2.4 at toolclips.py:199; DIG_CYCLE_SECONDS is 1.5 at gesture.ts:472. adultWork.ts:307–308 and :659 count progress using the latter while gltfClipLayers.ts:97 and :176 advance/repeat the former. Therefore progress-strike timing and visible tool motion can drift. Hardcoded grip offsets at toolclips.py:77–82 also need checking against every morphed hand.
